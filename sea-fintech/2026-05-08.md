# SEA Fintech Weekly — May 2–8, 2026

## 🎯 TL;DR

Indonesia and China launched a landmark cross-border QR payment linkage connecting QRIS with Alipay+ and UnionPay, instantly linking 40 million Indonesian merchants to Chinese consumers — the biggest ASEAN payment interoperability move of the year. Meanwhile, Grab posted blowout Q1 results ($955M revenue, +24% YoY) with Financial Services as its fastest-growing segment at +43%, and Asia's stablecoin landscape crystallized around a bank-anchored regulatory model that's now the de facto standard from Singapore to Bangkok.

---

## 🏦 NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**Grab Financial Services accelerates toward breakeven.** Grab's Q1 2026 earnings showed Financial Services revenue hitting $107M (+43% YoY), making it the company's fastest-growing segment. CFO Peter Oey confirmed new banking products are coming and the segment remains on track to break even by year-end. Full-year guidance: $4.04–4.10B revenue, $700–720M adjusted EBITDA. The Iran conflict-driven fuel price spike hasn't dented demand yet.

**GoTyme Bank hits 1 million app migrations.** The Philippines' fastest-growing digital bank successfully migrated 1 million customers to its redesigned app, which hit #1 across both app stores. GoTyme (Gokongwei Group + Tyme Group) is now expanding globally — from Manila to Johannesburg to Hong Kong — proving the Tyme Group's multi-market playbook works.

**SEA digital banks rethink core banking for AI.** A major Fintech News Singapore analysis flagged the growing tension between AI ambitions and legacy infrastructure. BFSIs are expected to spend $97B on AI by 2027, yet most initiatives aren't making it past controlled environments. The bottleneck isn't ambition — it's that core banking systems can't support AI-native workflows. This is the real opening for next-gen core banking providers in the region.

**Mox Bank (HK) breaks even in Q1 2026.** While not SEA proper, Hong Kong's Standard Chartered-backed virtual bank hitting profitability is a signal for the region's digital bank economics trajectory. If Mox can do it in 4 years, SEA digital banks licensed in 2022–2023 should be watching the clock.

**Singapore's digital bank landscape stabilizes at 5 players.** GXS Bank (Grab/Singtel), MariBank (Sea Limited), Trust Bank (StanChart/FairPrice), ANEXT Bank (Ant Financial), and Greenlink Digital Bank. Sea Limited reports Q1 earnings next week (May 12) — watch for MariBank updates. Sea's stock trading at $88.64.

**Philippines remains the most competitive digital bank market in SEA.** Six BSP-licensed digital banks (GoTyme, UnionDigital, OFBank, Tonik, UNObank, Maya Bank) plus GCash's GSave. Competition is fierce on deposit rates — Tonik maintains its 4.6/5 app rating with 112K reviews. InstaPay + PESONet processed a combined $401.8B (₱24.74T) in 2025, up 42% YoY, with 4.66 billion InstaPay transactions.

## 💸 PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**Indonesia-China cross-border QR payment linkage goes live (THE BIG ONE).** Bank Indonesia and PBOC officially launched QRIS-China interoperability on May 4, connecting 40M+ Indonesian merchants (mostly MSMEs) with Chinese consumers via Alipay+ and UnionPay International. Chinese users of Alipay and UnionPay App can pay at any QRIS merchant; Indonesian e-wallets can scan 80M+ Alipay/UnionPay QR codes in China. No new hardware needed for Indonesian merchants. Chinese visitor arrivals to Indonesia hit 1.34M in 2025 (6-year high), making this commercially significant immediately. This follows the Vietnam-South Korea QR linkage launched April 23.

**ASEAN+3 cross-border payment connectivity matures.** Business Times Singapore published a deep analysis noting strong progress across the ASEAN+3 bloc (ASEAN + China, Japan, South Korea) on payment interoperability, though gaps remain. The QRIS-China linkage is the latest in a cascade: Thailand-Singapore PromptPay-PayNow was first, now QR links are proliferating bilaterally before a true multilateral network emerges.

**Asia cross-border payments market projected at $24T by 2033.** A strategic outlook report projects retail cross-border payment outflows from Asia-Pacific will surge from $13.5T (2025) to $24T by 2033. Thailand is positioning itself as a regional leader in this space — not coincidentally where Money20/20 Asia just took place.

**Aleta Planet secures BNM licenses in Malaysia.** The Singapore-based fintech obtained both a Money Services Business Class B license and an E-Money Issuer license from Bank Negara Malaysia, enabling remittance and e-money services. Malaysia becomes Aleta Planet's second ASEAN hub. This signals BNM's continued openness to regional fintech players entering the Malaysian market.

**India's UPI goes global, reaches Vietnam.** UPI payment technology is now live across Singapore, Vietnam, France, UAE, and more — positioning India's real-time payment rail as a competitor to the ASEAN QR interoperability network. For SEA, this adds yet another cross-border payment rail to the increasingly crowded landscape.

## 📋 REGULATORY & POLICY

**Asia converges on bank-anchored stablecoin regulation.** Insignia VC published a landmark analysis showing that across Singapore, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Bangkok, and Seoul, regulators have converged on placing stablecoin issuance inside the banking perimeter — not in sandboxes or grey zones. Hong Kong issued its first two stablecoin licenses (HSBC and Anchorpoint Financial/StanChart consortium) in April. MAS confirmed draft stablecoin legislation coming in 2026. OJK (Indonesia) and BOT (Thailand) are building their own frameworks. The message: stablecoins in Asia are infrastructure, not experimentation.

**BNM holds rates at 2.75%.** All 28 economists in the Reuters poll correctly predicted Bank Negara would hold its overnight policy rate, the fifth consecutive hold since cutting to 2.75% in July 2025. Stable rates are good for fintech lending margins.

**BNM penalizes Zurich Malaysia for sanctions breaches.** Two Zurich entities paid RM1.56M combined for failing to screen customers against domestic sanctions lists. Signal: BNM is getting tougher on compliance — fintechs operating in Malaysia should take note.

**Money20/20 Asia Policy20 convenes 80+ regulators.** The dedicated policy track at Money20/20 Asia (Bangkok, April 21–23) brought together regulators to discuss "sovereign intelligence in global finance" — code for how Asian regulators are building their own financial infrastructure rather than defaulting to Western rails.

## 💰 FUNDING & DEALS

**Grab acquired Stash (US digital investment platform) — announced February 2026.** This deal extends Grab's financial services into wealth management, leveraging Stash's technology for SEA consumers. A clear signal Grab is building a super-app financial ecosystem, not just payments.

**Aleta Planet — BNM licensing (no funding disclosed).** While not a traditional funding round, securing dual licenses in Malaysia represents significant regulatory capital for this Singapore-based payments company.

**Movement invests in Stableyard** for stablecoin commerce infrastructure across SEA corridors. Movement's M1 network powers neobanks and fintechs across high-demand payment corridors including Southeast Asia.

**ADB commits $70B for energy and digital infrastructure through 2035.** The Asia-Pacific Digital Highway component will fund digital corridors, data infrastructure, and "AI-ready economies" — with a dedicated Regional Connectivity Fund for Power in Southeast Asia (RCF) launched in partnership with ASEAN governments. This is macro infrastructure that underpins everything fintech.

**Note:** SEA-specific fintech funding was relatively quiet this week compared to the US market. The global VC rebound is real (more late-stage mega-rounds, renewed IPO interest), but SEA-specific deals are being announced at a slower cadence. Watch for a pickup around Asia Tech x Singapore (May 20–22).

## 🌏 COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT

### Indonesia — The QR Payment Superpower

Indonesia dominated the week's headlines and for good reason. The country is becoming the center of gravity for ASEAN payment interoperability.

**By the numbers:**
- QRIS: 57M users, 39.3M merchants (H1 2025)
- QRIS transaction volume: $33.2B (IDR 579T) in 2025
- Fintech P2P lending: $4.6B outstanding (+31% YoY as of Feb 2025)
- Chinese visitor arrivals: 1.34M in 2025 (6-year high)

The QRIS-China linkage is strategically significant beyond payments. It deepens economic integration between China and Indonesia at a time when Beijing is stepping up financial connectivity across ASEAN. Indonesia is simultaneously the world's 4th most populous country, has the largest Muslim population (Islamic fintech opportunity), and is hosting Finance 2045 and World AI Show in Jakarta.

**OJK is quietly building stablecoin rules** as part of the broader Asian regulatory convergence. Indonesia's fintech P2P lending sector grew 31% YoY — one of the few markets where alternative lending is growing this fast under regulatory supervision.

### Vietnam — The Dark Horse Accelerates

Vietnam's interbank electronic payment system processed nearly 130 quadrillion VND in Q1 2026 alone (+70% YoY). The country is building its own international financial center (VIFC-HCMC targeting $10B in capital in 2026, Da Nang as a fintech/digital assets sandbox). The Vietnam-South Korea QR payment linkage launched April 23 adds another corridor. Vietnam is also now live with India's UPI.

## 🔮 TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. The bilateral QR web becomes the de facto ASEAN payment network.** Indonesia-China, Vietnam-South Korea, Thailand-Singapore, plus UPI expanding into the region. Rather than waiting for a multilateral framework, countries are building a web of bilateral links that achieves the same outcome. Expect Indonesia-Japan and Thailand-China QR links next.

**2. Stablecoin regulation will reshape SEA remittance corridors.** Asia's bank-anchored stablecoin model means regulated stablecoin rails will compete directly with traditional remittance. Movement's investment in Stableyard for SEA corridors, Bitget launching USDT QR payments, and Western Union selecting Fireblocks for USDPT infrastructure all point the same direction. The $24T cross-border market is the prize.

**3. Core banking replacement cycle begins.** SEA digital banks are hitting the wall between AI ambitions and legacy infrastructure. The $97B BFSI AI spend by 2027 will increasingly flow into core banking modernization rather than flashy front-end AI features. Watch for Thought Machine, Mambu, and SDK.finance gaining share in the region.

**4. Grab's financial services breakeven will trigger a wave.** If Grab Financial Services hits breakeven by year-end as guided, it validates the super-app financial services model and puts pressure on competitors (GCash/Mynt, Sea/MariBank) to accelerate their own paths to profitability.

## 💡 OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. MSME cross-border payment enablement.** The QRIS-China linkage specifically benefits MSMEs — 40M merchants get Chinese consumer access without new hardware. But the value-added services layer (FX management, working capital, analytics) for these MSMEs is wide open. A neobank that wraps cross-border payment acceptance with MSME lending and financial management could own this segment.

**2. AI-native core banking for SEA.** The article on SEA digital banks struggling with AI integration highlights a gap: no one has built a core banking system designed from the ground up for AI-native operations in SEA's regulatory context. Most are bolting AI onto legacy stacks. First mover advantage is still available here — especially for Indonesia and Philippines where volumes are highest.

**3. Stablecoin-powered remittance for the unbanked corridor.** Vietnam-Philippines and Indonesia-Malaysia are massive migrant worker remittance corridors. With Asia's bank-anchored stablecoin frameworks maturing, there's an opening for a licensed stablecoin remittance product that undercuts traditional players on cost while meeting regulatory requirements. The infrastructure pieces (Movement/Stableyard, Rain/Mastercard, Alipay+) exist — someone needs to assemble them for these specific corridors.
