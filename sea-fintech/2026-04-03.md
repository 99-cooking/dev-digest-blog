# SEA Fintech Weekly — Mar 28 – Apr 3, 2026

## 🎯 TL;DR

Thailand's virtual banking race officially kicked off as SCBX formed BankX Bank with Korea's Kakao Bank and China's WeBank — the first consortium to formally incorporate ahead of BOT licensing. Meanwhile, stablecoin payments are going "invisible" across SEA as Nium launched stablecoin card issuance, SQRIL expanded to Thailand and Cambodia, and Vietnam introduced a landmark 0.1% crypto transaction tax to pull trading onshore. The cross-border QR payments web keeps expanding: Korea-Indonesia QRIS went live April 1, and Cambodia-Singapore completed full interoperability.

## 🏦 NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**SCBX Forms BankX Bank for Thailand Virtual Bank Bid**
SCBX has formally established BankX Bank Co. Ltd with partners Kakao Bank (10% stake) and WeBank (technology partner). SCBX holds 90% initially, plans to raise registered capital to THB 5 billion (~$140M) within 2026 for the Ministry of Finance licence application. SCBX expects to begin virtual bank operations by Q4 2026. This is the first of Thailand's three approved virtual bank consortiums to formally incorporate — a signal of real urgency. They simultaneously dropped their THB 31 billion Home Credit Vietnam acquisition to focus resources.
→ *Why it matters:* Thailand's virtual bank race is the region's most closely watched new-entrant story. SCBX pairing Korean UX (Kakao) with Chinese scale-tech (WeBank) is a potent combination.

**Malaysia's Five Digital Banks Hit 2.4M Customers, RM4.2B Deposits**
BNM's Annual Report 2025 revealed Malaysia's five digital banks (GXBank, Boost Bank, AEON Bank, KAF Digital, YTL Digital) collectively reached 2.4 million customers and RM4.2 billion ($1.04B) in deposits by end-2025. BNM simultaneously launched the DIME (Digital Inclusion Measurement) framework to track whether digital banks are actually serving underbanked segments rather than just cannibalising incumbent customers.
→ *Why it matters:* $1B+ in deposits within ~18 months of launch shows real traction. The DIME framework signals regulators will hold digital banks accountable on inclusion, not just growth metrics.

**UNO Digital Bank (Philippines) Hits Operating Breakeven**
The four-year-old Philippine digital bank announced it reached operating break-even before provisions in February 2026 — a critical milestone for any challenger bank. UNO is positioning itself for growth-stage scaling.
→ *Why it matters:* First Philippine digital bank to publicly claim breakeven. Sets the profitability benchmark for peers like GoTyme, Tonik, and Maya Bank.

**BigPay Undergoes Restructuring Across Three Markets**
AirAsia-linked fintech BigPay is going through a restructuring round impacting senior leaders and tech staff across Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand. The restructuring follows Capital A's appointment of former CIMB leader Effendy Shahul Hamid as Deputy CEO, suggesting a strategic reset.
→ *Why it matters:* BigPay has long been AirAsia's fintech bet. Layoffs at leadership level signal either a pivot or a tightening runway — watch for acquisition interest.

**Western Union Completes Dash Acquisition from Singtel**
Western Union closed its acquisition of Singapore digital wallet Dash (SingCash Pte Ltd) from Singtel on April 2, marking WU's first digital wallet expansion in APAC. The deal received all required regulatory approvals.
→ *Why it matters:* Legacy remittance giant buying a SEA wallet is defensive positioning. WU needs a consumer-facing product as stablecoin rails threaten its corridor business.

## 💸 PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**Korea-Indonesia Cross-Border QR Payments Go Live (April 1)**
Bank of Korea and Bank Indonesia launched bilateral QR code-based payments, allowing Indonesians to use QRIS in South Korea and Koreans to pay in Indonesia via domestic finance apps. BOK plans to expand the service to Vietnam, Thailand, Singapore, and India.
→ *Why it matters:* QRIS is becoming the de facto ASEAN interoperability standard. Korea linking in from outside ASEAN validates the protocol's extensibility.

**Cambodia-Singapore Complete Full QR Payment Interoperability**
NBC and Liquid Group launched Phase 2 of the Bakong-RoamQR linkage on March 30, enabling Singaporean travelers to pay via KHQR at merchants across Cambodia without currency exchange. Phase 1 (Cambodia→Singapore) was already live.
→ *Why it matters:* Full bidirectional QR payments between two countries with vastly different banking penetration levels. This is the inclusion use case working in practice.

**Malaysia's E-Payments Surge 25% to 18.4 Billion Transactions**
BNM reported e-payment transactions hit 18.4 billion in 2025, up 25% YoY, with a CAGR of 17% since 2022 — consistently surpassing the Financial Sector Blueprint 2022-2026 targets.
→ *Why it matters:* Malaysia is quietly becoming one of SEA's most cashless economies. TNG eWallet just opened its first permanent physical service hub in KL — a sign digital wallets now need offline presence at scale.

**Maybank Completes On-Chain Tokenised Cross-Border Payment**
Maybank executed its first on-chain transaction involving tokenised deposits, FX conversion (MYR→SGD), and cross-border payment using its own permissioned blockchain on March 25. The pilot was conducted with Yinson Holdings.
→ *Why it matters:* This isn't a startup experiment — it's ASEAN's largest bank by assets running real tokenised corporate treasury workflows. If Maybank productionises this, it changes the MYR-SGD corridor permanently.

**Asia Remains World's Most Wallet-Led Region (Worldpay)**
New Worldpay data shows APAC continues to lead globally in digital wallet usage, with A2A payments accelerating. QR-based payment methods identified as the primary driver.

## 📋 REGULATORY & POLICY

**Vietnam Introduces 0.1% Crypto Transaction Tax**
Vietnam's Ministry of Finance issued a circular imposing a 0.1% tax on the value of each crypto asset transfer. Crypto assets are exempted from VAT. The deliberately low rate is designed to pull trading onto licensed domestic platforms rather than offshore exchanges.
→ *Why it matters:* Vietnam was previously a regulatory grey zone for crypto despite having one of the world's highest adoption rates. This is formalisation, not crackdown — bullish for compliant exchanges.

**BNM Backs New Consumer Credit Commission to Regulate BNPL**
Bank Negara Malaysia announced support for a new Consumer Credit Commission that will bring BNPL providers under formal regulatory oversight for the first time. BNPL transaction volumes have surged to RM11.9 billion ($2.9B) in Malaysia.
→ *Why it matters:* SEA's first dedicated BNPL regulatory body. Expect this to set precedent for BSP, OJK, and BOT to follow.

**BNM Updates RMiT Framework with Stricter Authentication Rules**
New requirements mandate passkeys, adaptive MFA, device binding, and risk-based authentication for all banks and fintech apps operating in Malaysia. Compliance timeline not yet announced.

**Airwallex Secures Full Malaysia Licences**
Airwallex received BNM approval for both e-money issuing and Class A payment licences, enabling full commercial launch of multi-currency payment services in Malaysia.

**Vietnam Police Launch Crypto Fraud Probe**
Authorities in Hanoi launched a large-scale investigation into alleged cryptocurrency fraud linked to ONUS, a platform claiming 7M+ users. The platform's VNDC stablecoin (pegged to Vietnamese dong) is under scrutiny.

## 🔮 TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. Stablecoins Are Going "Invisible" in SEA Payments**
Between Nium's stablecoin card issuance platform, SQRIL expanding to Thailand/Cambodia, StraitsX's XSGD dominance (70%+ non-USD stablecoin share in SEA), and Maya integrating crypto into retail — stablecoins are becoming backend plumbing. Consumers won't know they're using crypto; they'll just see faster, cheaper payments. The Vietnam 0.1% tax and ONUS crackdown create a carrot-and-stick dynamic that will accelerate this trend.

**2. QR Interoperability Is Creating an ASEAN Payment Union**
Korea-Indonesia, Cambodia-Singapore, and the upcoming ASEAN-wide platform (expected 2026) are creating something that looks increasingly like a payments single market. The key unlock: once QR corridors are bilateral and bidirectional, multilateral follows naturally.

**3. BNPL Is Moving from Online Checkout to Physical Retail**
Atome in 1,300 Jollibee stores. Instalments on fast food. This is the moment BNPL stops being an e-commerce feature and becomes embedded in daily life — which is exactly when regulators (like Malaysia's new Consumer Credit Commission) step in.

**4. Indonesia's Austerity Crunch Will Squeeze Fintech**
Indonesia's budget was based on $70/barrel oil; Brent hit $100. The government is cutting up to IDR 190 trillion ($11.2B) in spending. Reduced government digital initiatives, tighter consumer spending, and potential subsidy costs will pressure fintech growth in H2 2026. Watch for consolidation.

## 💡 OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Treasury-as-a-Service for SEA Corporates**
Maybank's tokenised deposit pilot and Finmo's multi-country treasury OS show that corporate treasury management in SEA is still archaic. There's a gap between what incumbents offer (batch processing, T+1 settlement) and what corporates need (real-time, multi-currency, programmable). A neobank offering embedded treasury tools to mid-market SEA businesses could own a lucrative niche.

**2. BNPL Compliance Infrastructure**
With Malaysia launching a dedicated BNPL regulator and volumes at RM11.9B, every BNPL provider in SEA will need compliance tooling — credit risk scoring, regulatory reporting, debt counselling integration. The providers exist for banks; they barely exist for BNPL-specific use cases.

**3. Stablecoin-to-Fiat Rails for Remittance Corridors**
SQRIL is proving the model: stablecoin-funded QR payments at local merchants. The Vietnam-Philippines and Indonesia-Singapore remittance corridors are massive ($20B+/year combined) and still dominated by legacy players charging 3-7% fees. A neobank with stablecoin-backed remittance at <1% fees and instant QR cashout could capture meaningful share.
