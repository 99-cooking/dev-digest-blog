# SEA Fintech Weekly — April 25 – May 1, 2026

## 🎯 TL;DR

Indonesia and China launched cross-border QRIS — the biggest QR interoperability deal yet, connecting 280M+ Indonesian digital wallets to Chinese tourists and vice versa. Trust Bank became Singapore's first digital bank to hit profitability. Vietnam greenlit its first regulated crypto exchange pilot with $380M minimum capital requirements. And Visa's "Agentic Ready" programme landed in APAC with 50+ partners, signaling that AI-agent-initiated payments are no longer theoretical. The theme of the week: SEA fintech is graduating from user acquisition to monetization — and the infrastructure layer is being rebuilt around AI commerce and stablecoins.

## 🏦 NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**Trust Bank hits profitability in March 2026 — Singapore's first digital bank to do so.** The Standard Chartered–FairPrice joint venture turned profitable just over three years post-launch, also winning "World's Best Pure-Play Digital Bank for CX" at the Digital CX Awards in Singapore. The playbook: high-frequency grocery/retail integration drove deposits, not flashy interest rates. This is a signal to every digital bank in SEA that the path to profitability runs through embedded everyday commerce, not rate wars.

**GXS Bank (Grab-Singtel JV) narrows FY2025 losses to S$208.1M, loan book triples to S$1B.** CEO Lai Pei-Si plans to keep scaling in 2026. GXS also launched a property-backed SME loan (up to S$2M) in partnership with Funding Societies — the first time a Singapore digital bank has offered secured lending. GXS now has 280,000 SME users. The pivot from consumer deposits to SME credit is accelerating.

**Tonik CEO Greg Krasnov publishes a manifesto: "The next decade of EM fintech will be won on credit, not users."** Published via Insignia VC, the piece argues that 75%+ of neobanks globally remain unprofitable because payment-led models generate $0.30–0.60 ARPAC while credit-led models yield $10–20. He notes GCash took $393M from MUFG on a credit thesis, Maya only became profitable after pivoting to lending, and Grab Financial disbursed $3.2B in loans in 2025 (+47% YoY). This is the definitive articulation of why SEA's next wave belongs to credit-first players.

**Maya wins "Neobank of the Year 2026" at The Asset Triple A Awards**, recognized for fraud protection and its profitable pivot to digital banking and credit in the Philippines.

**Over half of Filipinos remain unbanked despite digital shift** — PIDS study finds account ownership rose from 29% (2019) to 56% (2021) but plateaued. Digital payments now = 57.4% of retail transactions. Structural barriers persist: lack of funds, high costs, low trust. AI adoption for credit scoring is growing but uneven — large players lead, cooperatives lag.

**SEA leads Asia in fintech density at 14 companies per million people** (UnaFinancial study). Singapore is the extreme outlier at 619 fintech firms per million. South Asia trails at 9, Central Asia at 5.8, East Asia at 4.8. The 300x gap between densest and least dense markets signals massive runway in underserved corridors.

**DBS/POSB commits S$10M in cashback** for Singaporean consumers (Aug–Dec 2026) and is stepping up GenAI support for SMEs through its Spark GenAI platform — expanding AI adoption beyond banking into business intelligence for small merchants.

## 💸 PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**Indonesia–China launch cross-border QRIS — the week's headline deal.** Bank Indonesia Governor Perry Warjiyo unveiled bilateral QR interoperability on April 30, enabling Indonesian travelers in China and Chinese visitors in Indonesia to scan and pay via QRIS/UnionPay/Alipay. Uses direct rupiah-yuan settlement — no USD intermediary. BI reported 14.39 billion digital transactions in Q1 2026 (+33.76% YoY), with QRIS transactions surging 119%. BI targeting QRIS coverage in 8 countries by end-2026. Indonesia's coordinating minister also announced QRIS will extend to all APEC member states.

**Vietnam–South Korea cross-border QR payments go live.** BIDV, NAPAS, KEB Hana Bank and GLN International launched the service on April 23, enabling Korean travelers to pay Vietnamese merchants via QR. GLN also announced direct integration into Vietnam's national QR payment infrastructure — a first for a Korean payment network.

**Malaysia formally joins the ASEAN cross-border QR payment network** alongside Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand. The four-country network is now the world's most advanced regional QR interoperability system, primarily serving intra-ASEAN tourism and trade flows.

**PayNow nickname feature being removed in Singapore from June 6** — ABS announced the anti-scam measure will replace custom display names with masked registered account names. No user action required. Privacy-security tradeoff that signals Singapore's continued regulatory tightening on fraud.

**Ant International now connects 150M+ merchants with 2B+ consumer accounts globally**, announced at Money20/20 Asia. Plans to roll out Islamic finance programs in SEA and Alipay+ cross-border QR payments in Saudi Arabia (with SAMA/mada). Introduced new AI-powered tools for agentic commerce.

## 📋 REGULATORY & POLICY

**Vietnam launches first regulated crypto exchange pilot — Q2 2026.** Under the new Law on Digital Technology Industry (effective Jan 1, 2026), the State Securities Commission began accepting license applications on January 20. Five-year pilot program. Minimum capital requirement: 10 trillion VND (~$380M). VPBank-linked CAEX is the frontrunner, with OKX Ventures and HashKey Capital confirming capital injections. Vietnam's estimated $230B in annual crypto volume is going onshore.

**OJK (Indonesia) licensed fintech lenders drop to 94 companies** in April 2026, continuing a consolidation trend. 951 illegal lending apps shut down. Meanwhile, OJK is calling for fiscal stimulus for capital markets and encouraging fintech diversification.

**BNM (Malaysia) fines Zurich Insurance units RM1.56M** for sanctions screening failures — both entities failed to update domestic sanctions databases and properly screen customers under terrorism financing rules. Signal that AML/CFT enforcement is tightening across SEA even for legacy insurers.

**Singapore: MAS now has 3 digital full banks, 20 full banks, 95 wholesale banks** per latest records. The regulatory framework is stable — focus shifting from licensing to profitability expectations.

**VIDA (Indonesia's digital identity network) warns at Money20/20 Asia** that traditional identity security approaches are no longer sufficient for fintech, calling for AI-native identity verification.

## 🌏 COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT

### Indonesia: The QR Payments Superpower

Indonesia is having a moment. The QRIS-China launch is the crown jewel, but the broader picture is staggering:
- **14.39B digital transactions in Q1 2026** (+33.76% YoY)
- **QRIS transactions up 119%** — adoption exploding among MSMEs
- **~1,200 fintech companies** — largest in SEA by count
- **26 fintechs registered in Bank Indonesia's sandbox**
- QRIS expansion target: **8 countries by end-2026**, APEC-wide eventually
- Fintech lending consolidating: down to 94 OJK-licensed lenders (from 100+)

Indonesia's strategy is clear: use QRIS as the rails for both domestic financial inclusion and international trade settlement, bypassing USD where possible. The direct rupiah-yuan settlement mechanism is a geopolitical statement as much as a fintech one.

### Singapore: The Profitability Phase

Singapore's digital banks are crossing the profitability threshold:
- **Trust Bank:** First to profit (March 2026), 3 years post-launch
- **GXS Bank:** Losses narrowing, loan book at S$1B, secured lending launched
- **MariBank:** Hybrid model with UOB institutional backing

Meanwhile, Singapore remains the regulatory and innovation hub:
- **Visa Agentic Ready** launched with 13 Singapore issuers
- **Money20/20 Asia** drew record attendance to Bangkok but Singapore was the deal-making center
- **DBS** committing S$10M cashback + GenAI for SMEs
- **PayNow** anti-scam tightening

## 🔮 TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. Agentic commerce is the next payment frontier.** Visa launched Agentic Ready in APAC with 50+ partners. Stripe unveiled Machine Payments Protocol for AI agent transactions. Ant International released AI tools for agentic commerce at Money20/20. Within 12–18 months, expect SEA super-apps (Grab, GoTo, Sea) to integrate agent-initiated payments into their ecosystems. The race isn't to build AI — it's to build the payment rails AI agents will use.

**2. Stablecoins are going mainstream in SEA remittance.** Western Union launching USDPT (Solana-based stablecoin) in May. Visa's stablecoin settlement hit $7B annualized run rate across 9 blockchains. Vietnam's crypto exchange pilot legitimizes on-chain finance. Expect Philippines and Indonesia — the region's largest remittance corridors — to see stablecoin settlement pilots within 2026.

**3. Credit-led neobanks will consolidate the market.** Krasnov's thesis is playing out: GCash pivoting to credit, Maya profitable on lending, Grab Financial at $3.2B disbursed. Payment-only fintechs face an existential question. Watch for M&A as credit-first players acquire user bases from payment-led ones.

**4. QR interoperability is becoming ASEAN's killer infrastructure.** Indonesia–China, Vietnam–Korea, the 4-country ASEAN network. QRIS targeting 8 countries + APEC. This is the closest thing to a pan-Asian payment layer — and it's happening bottom-up through bilateral deals, not top-down through a single protocol.

## 💡 OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Islamic digital banking in SEA is wide open.** Ant International just announced Islamic finance programs for SEA. Malaysia (BNM) has the regulatory framework. Indonesia has 230M+ Muslims. Yet there is no dominant digital-first Islamic neobank in the region. A shariah-compliant neobank with embedded financing (murabaha, musharakah) targeting Indonesia and Malaysia could capture an underserved segment worth $50B+.

**2. AI-native credit scoring for the 290M unbanked.** The Philippines PIDS study confirms traditional credit infrastructure can't reach 50%+ of adults. The winning model: alternative data (e-commerce history, mobile usage, utility payments) processed through foundation models. Revolut's PRAGMA and Nubank's nuFormer both prove this works at scale. SEA needs its own version — purpose-built for informal economies.

**3. SME embedded finance beyond lending.** GXS's Funding Societies partnership and DBS's GenAI tools for SMEs hint at the opportunity: small businesses need more than loans — they need integrated invoicing, tax, insurance, and treasury management in one platform. The SEA SME funding gap is $100B+. Whoever builds the "Stripe for SEA SMEs" captures the most defensible niche in the region.
