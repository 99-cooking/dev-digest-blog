# SEA Fintech Weekly — Apr 11–17, 2026

## TL;DR
Money20/20 Asia lands in Bangkok next week (Apr 21–23), making this the most event-dense week of the year for SEA fintech. The headline data drop: ASEAN cross-border QR payments hit $716.4M in 2025 across 36.2M transactions — the clearest proof yet that real-time payment interoperability is working at scale. Meanwhile, Malaysia's digital banks hit their BNM deadline for basic banking services, the Philippines' digital banks dominated Forbes' 2026 Best Banks list, and MAS is pushing stablecoins toward systemic-risk monitoring frameworks. Infrastructure is the story now — not apps.

## NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**Philippines digital banks sweep Forbes 2026 Best Banks list.** GoTyme Bank placed #2 and Maya Bank #5 in the Philippines rankings, alongside MariBank — all three are digital-only banks cracking the top five ahead of traditional incumbents. This is a legitimacy milestone: digital banks aren't "challenger" brands anymore in the PH, they're just banks. GoTyme (backed by Gokongwei Group + Tyme Group) has been on a tear with its aggressive savings rate strategy.

**Maya eyes 2026 IPO — dual listing in US then PSE.** Maya (formerly PayMaya) is targeting a dual listing, first in the US and then on the Philippine Stock Exchange in H2 2026. With its wallet + banking + lending stack and deep integration into the PH payments ecosystem, this would be the first major SEA digital bank IPO since Sea's listing. A successful Maya IPO would reset valuation benchmarks for the entire region.

**KAF Digital Bank meets BNM's April 1 deadline for basic banking services.** All five Malaysian digital bank licensees (GX Bank, Boost Bank, AEON Bank, KAF Digital Bank, and RYT Bank) were required to roll out basic banking services by April 1, 2026. KAF confirmed compliance. Malaysian digital banks are now scaling via ecosystem plays — AEON is leveraging its parent's motorcycle financing network; Boost Bank taps the Axiata telco ecosystem.

**Monee (Sea Limited) loan book crosses $9.2B.** Sea's fintech arm grew lending 80% YoY with an NPL ratio of just 1.1%. The "all can apply" shift from invite-only hasn't moved default rates — Shopee's transaction data is powering AI underwriting at a scale traditional banks can't match. With 37M credit users against Shopee's 400M active buyers, penetration is still under 10%. Revenue hit $3.8B at 25.6% operating margins.

**GCash at 94M users, but cash still king in Philippines.** Despite GCash's massive base (94M users, 6M+ merchants), cash still accounts for 42% of PH in-store transactions and 23% of e-commerce (cash-on-delivery). The 50% unbanked rate per World Bank 2024 data explains the persistence. The gap between wallet registration and wallet-as-primary-account remains the big challenge.

**Digital wallets overtake debit cards at POS in Singapore — for the first time.** According to Worldpay's Global Payments Report 2026, digital wallets captured 36% of POS value in Singapore in 2025, surpassing debit cards. GrabPay, ShopeePay, Apple Pay, and Google Pay lead the ecosystem. Credit cards still dominate at a 3:1 ratio over debit by value.

**Vietnam banking sector: 155M biometric verifications completed.** Vietnam's State Bank reported hundreds of millions of customer records standardised and nearly 155M verified through biometric authentication as part of its anti-fraud push. The SIMO system has issued warnings to over 3.7M customers as of April 12, 2026. Vietnam is projected to grow 7.2% in 2026 (ADB), making it the fastest-growing major SEA economy.

## PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**ASEAN cross-border QR payments hit $716.4M in 2025.** The 13th ASEAN Finance Ministers' and Central Bank Governors' Meeting (held in the Philippines) disclosed 36.2M transactions worth $716.4M in cross-border QR payments, with 29 QR and P2P instant payment linkages established. P2P transfers alone hit $305.7M across 1.6M transactions. ASEAN is building a regulatory comparison dashboard to reduce ambiguity across payment schemes.

**Korea-Indonesia cross-border QR payment goes live.** Korean travelers can now pay at Indonesian merchants via KB Star Banking app using QRIS, with fees around 1% and no card cloning risk. QRIS previously expanded to Japan (Aug 2025) and is now live in Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, and the Philippines. Indonesia's QR standard is becoming the de facto cross-border payment rail in the region.

**PayNow-PromptPay-UPI corridor: the anti-SWIFT triangle.** Singapore's PayNow is now bilaterally linked with Thailand's PromptPay and India's UPI, creating instant payment corridors that bypass traditional SWIFT infrastructure entirely. A company licensed in Singapore can effectively access three major Asian economies through real-time rails. This is the infrastructure play that defines 2026.

**Indonesia's cash-to-digital shift: fastest in the region.** Indonesia's POS cash share collapsed from 77% (2019) to 36% (2025), driven by BI-FAST and QRIS. With 57M users and 40M merchants on QRIS, the infrastructure is now at meaningful scale. Global Payments Report 2026 projects digital payments in SEA to exceed $1.5T this year.

**Atome launches BNPL card in Malaysia.** Singapore-based BNPL player Atome rolled out a physical BNPL card, bridging the gap between installment payments and traditional POS acceptance. This moves BNPL from e-commerce checkout into offline retail — a significant distribution unlock in markets where card penetration is still low.

## REGULATORY & POLICY

**MAS pushes for systemic-risk monitoring of stablecoins.** At the IMF Spring Meetings (Apr 13), MAS Assistant MD Tuang Lee Lim called for stablecoins to be assessed for systemic importance under internationally agreed standards (PFMI). This signals Singapore moving from "sandbox-friendly" to "systemically-aware" on stablecoins — a maturation of the regulatory posture.

**MAS expands crypto payment sandbox to include stablecoin settlement.** The sandbox now enables direct integration between stablecoin payments and traditional settlement, with audit trails for regulatory reporting. Expected to handle up to $4.5B in annual transaction volume by year-end. StraitsX (XUSD/XSGD) continues operating as a MAS-licensed Major Payment Institution.

**OJK issues new fintech and crypto regulations.** Indonesia's OJK released updated rules covering fintech lending and crypto assets, with AFTECH (Indonesian Fintech Association) responding positively. OJK has formally taken over crypto oversight, moving digital asset supervision deeper into the formal regulatory system. Meanwhile, OJK is running "Crypto Literacy Month" (Bulan Literasi Kripto) throughout April 2026 with the Blockchain Association of Indonesia.

**BSP announces relief measures amid Philippines energy emergency.** The Philippine central bank approved regulatory relief measures for banks and borrowers during the 2026 national energy emergency. Separately, BSP is making ASEAN regional payments connectivity a Priority Economic Deliverable under the Philippines' 2026 ASEAN chairmanship theme "Navigating Our Future, Together."

**Hong Kong issues first stablecoin licenses (HSBC + Anchorpoint).** While not SEA, this has direct regional implications. HKMA granted licenses under the new Stablecoins Ordinance effective April 10, 2026. Only 2 of 36 applicants approved — signaling very high bars. Both licensees have banking backgrounds and participated in CBDC experiments.

## FUNDING & DEALS

**Global fintech funding: $12B across 751 deals in Q1 2026.** Per Crunchbase, global fintech investment reached $12B through April 6. Y Combinator remains the most active fintech investor. SEA-specific deals were quieter this week, but the pipeline is building ahead of Money20/20 Asia.

**Collide Capital closes $95M Fund II** — targeting early-stage fintech and supply chain startups with $170M+ total AUM. While US-focused, the fund's supply chain thesis has SEA exposure.

**Maya IPO prep** — targeting dual US/PSE listing in 2026. No valuation disclosed yet, but given the 94M GCash user comp and Maya's banking license, expect significant interest from SEA-focused funds.

**Nubank sets up global HQ at Abu Dhabi's ADGM.** Not SEA, but Nubank's global expansion signals increased competitive pressure on SEA neobanks. After securing a conditional US national bank charter in Jan 2026, Nubank now has 70M+ customers globally. Its playbook (mobile-first, zero-fee, data-driven underwriting) is directly applicable to SEA markets.

## COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT: Philippines

The Philippines is having its moment. Between the Forbes rankings (3 digital banks in top 5), BSP's ASEAN chairmanship pushing payments connectivity, Maya's IPO plans, and GCash at 94M users, the PH fintech ecosystem is the most dynamic in SEA right now.

**Why it matters:** The Philippines has the highest unbanked rate in SEA (50% per World Bank), the highest cash-on-delivery rate globally (23% of e-commerce), and the highest remittance dependency (OFW remittances = ~10% of GDP). Every fintech thesis that matters — inclusion, payments, remittance, credit — converges here.

**Key tension:** GCash vs Maya is the defining rivalry. GCash has scale (94M users); Maya has the banking license, lending infrastructure, and IPO ambitions. GoTyme is the aggressive third player backed by South African digital banking pioneer Tyme Group. The BSP has been remarkably constructive — six digital bank licenses issued, clear regulatory frameworks, active promotion of interoperability (InstaPay, QR Ph).

**Watch next:** Maya's IPO timeline, BSP's ASEAN payments connectivity deliverables, and whether GCash parent Mynt finally files for its own listing.

## TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**Infrastructure > Apps.** The Business Times nailed it: "Payments infrastructure — not apps — will define South-East Asia fintech's next decade." QRIS, BI-FAST, PayNow-PromptPay, DuitNow — the real-time payment rails are the moats now. Building another wallet is pointless; plugging into the rails is everything.

**Stablecoins meet card rails.** Thredd, Reap, and Fireblocks are building infrastructure that lets fintech firms link stablecoin balances directly to card payments. Hong Kong's first stablecoin licenses (HSBC, Anchorpoint) and Singapore's expanded sandbox signal that regulated stablecoins are about to plug into existing payment networks — not replace them. Forbes reported stablecoins have out-processed Visa in settlement volume.

**AI-powered underwriting is the real moat.** Monee's 1.1% NPL ratio at $9.2B in loans, while shifting from invite-only to "all can apply," is the proof point. Shopee's behavioral data (purchase frequency, basket size, repayment history) feeds credit models that no traditional bank can replicate. Expect GCash, Maya, and GrabFin to double down on similar approaches.

**Money20/20 Asia (Apr 21–23, Bangkok) will set the narrative.** With 250 speakers from 39 countries, expect announcements around agentic payments, cross-border QR expansion, and embedded finance. The defining theme is "Hyperlocal Ecosystem Orchestration" — global tech, local relevance.

## OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**Remittance-to-credit pipeline in Philippines.** With OFW remittances at ~10% of GDP and 50% unbanked, there's a massive opportunity to convert regular remittance recipients into credit-scored borrowers. The data signal (regular, predictable income from overseas) is one of the strongest underwriting inputs possible — yet no player has fully productized this.

**QRIS-as-a-service for non-ASEAN markets.** QRIS expanded to Japan in Aug 2025 and Korea just went live. The opportunity to build middleware that helps non-ASEAN banks and wallets integrate with ASEAN QR payment networks is wide open. Think "Stripe for ASEAN cross-border QR."

**Digital financial literacy tools for the 70%+ underbanked.** ASEAN just published its Digital Financial Literacy Core Competency Framework (Feb 2026). The gap between wallet registration and actual financial product adoption is massive. The next wave of winners will be those who move users from "I have GCash" to "I have savings, insurance, and a credit score" — fintech education as a product, not a CSR afterthought.
