# SEA Fintech Weekly — March 14–20, 2026

## TL;DR

Indonesia's fintech lending sector is undergoing a brutal consolidation as OJK sanctions 22 lenders and major players like Maucash exit the market — this is the sector growing up. Meanwhile, GCash launched international remittance transfers to 16 countries, Binance secured full Thai licensing with a baht on-ramp coming Q3, and stablecoin infrastructure is rapidly becoming the preferred alternative to correspondent banking for SEA cross-border payments.

## NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**GoTyme Bank hits 12 million customers globally.** The "phygital" challenger bank — operating across the Philippines, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Vietnam, Singapore, and South Africa — announced it has surpassed 12M users. They claim to be Africa's first profitable digital bank in under five years, and their multi-market playbook (kiosks + app + retail partners) is proving the hybrid distribution model works where pure-digital struggles with trust.

**Sea Limited posts $22.94B annual revenue, fintech segment under scrutiny.** SE reported Q4 2025 results with $6.85B quarterly revenue and $1.58B annual net income. Analysts are watching whether SeaMoney can scale lending profitably. Stock saw institutional buying (Fred Alger added 521K shares) despite a 6.5% post-earnings dip. Consensus: "Moderate Buy" with $1.14 EPS expected for Q1 2026.

**Grab Financial expected to hit EBITDA breakeven in H2 2026.** Seeking Alpha analysis highlights that Grab's fintech and advertising segments are the underappreciated growth engines. The super app's lending, insurance, and payments verticals are finally approaching scale economics. If they hit breakeven this year, it validates the super-app-as-bank thesis for SEA.

**Revolut now at 70M+ customers globally, UK bank license fully activated.** Revolut received PRA approval to exit its mobilisation phase and operate as a fully licensed UK bank. This "paves the way for lending and other products" — and strengthens their hand as they expand in SEA (already live in Singapore, exploring Philippines/Vietnam). The UK license gives institutional credibility that matters for Asian regulators.

**Philippines digital payments hit 57.4% of transaction volume.** BSP data confirms digital retail payments surpassed the government's target range in 2024. The Philippines has compressed "years of digital behaviour change into a very short window" — 137M cellular connections (117% of population), 98M internet users, 83.8% internet penetration. GCash claims 6M+ partner merchants.

**FT-Statista Asia-Pacific fastest-growing fintechs list released.** Singapore dominates with 12 entries, led by Aspire (1,772% growth, $44.7M revenue), Storepay (1,722%), and Syfe (633%, $10B+ AUM). Indonesia's DurianPay hit 1,626%. Malaysia's PolicyStreet (insurtech) grew 1,311%. Funding Societies clocked 174% growth to $54.4M revenue. The list confirms SME finance and embedded payments are the hottest verticals.

**ADB and GCash's Fuse Financing ink landmark $30M MSME loan program.** This is the first partnership of its kind in ASEAN fintech — a multilateral development bank directly funding a super-app's lending arm. The deal targets micro, small, and medium enterprises in the Philippines, validating GCash's evolution from wallet to full financial services platform.

## PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**GCash launches international money transfers to 16 countries.** Users can now send PHP directly from their wallets to overseas bank accounts, cards, and e-wallets in the US, China, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, UAE, Saudi Arabia, and more. Recipients get local currency. This targets the Philippines' massive $40B annual remittance market — and positions GCash as a direct competitor to Western Union and Wise on the most lucrative corridor in SEA.

**QRIS cross-border expansion: Japanese tourists can now pay in Indonesia.** Bank Indonesia authorized QRIS acceptance for Japanese visitors, eliminating the need for currency exchange. This follows the broader ASEAN QR interoperability push linking QRIS, PromptPay, PayNow, and DuitNow. Real-time cross-border payments are being enabled by multilateral linkages between national instant payment systems.

**TransFi raises $19M Series A for stablecoin-powered cross-border payments.** The company is targeting SEA, South Asia, Middle East, LatAm, and Africa with stablecoin rails that replace correspondent banking. Projecting $5B in processed transactions by 2026 with AI-enhanced B2B tools. This is the stablecoin-as-infrastructure thesis becoming real — SEA remittance fees can drop from 6.49% to under 1%.

**Asia-Pacific cross-border payments market: $12.8T in 2024, projected $23.8T by 2032.** Digital payment transaction value expected to hit $8.63T by 2030. The convergence of ISO 20022 messaging, CBDC pilots, and instant payment system linkages is fundamentally reshaping how money moves across ASEAN borders.

## REGULATORY & POLICY

**Indonesia OJK sanctions 22 fintech lenders, triggers market consolidation.** The regulator disclosed that 22 digital lenders were sanctioned in February, with 9 of 95 licensed operators still failing to meet the Rp12.5B minimum equity requirement. The 90-day delinquency ratio (TWP90) climbed to 4.33% from 2.52% a year earlier. Multiple exits underway: Maucash (Astra/WeLab-backed) approved to withdraw, Pinjam Modal surrendering license, Danakini restructured. This is the end of Indonesia's P2P land-grab era.

**MAS halts new crypto licenses in Singapore.** After a June 2025 deadline, the Monetary Authority of Singapore has effectively frozen new crypto licensing, enforcing strict capital, compliance, and consumer protection requirements. Only a handful of firms remain licensed. Singapore is choosing quality over quantity — a clear signal to the market.

**Binance secures full licensing in Thailand.** Gulf Binance (JV with Thai billionaire Sarath Ratanavadi's Gulf Innova) received approval as exchange, broker, and custodian. THB fiat on-ramp launching Q3 2026. Bangkok will host a regional hub with developer events and regulatory workshops. Thailand is positioning itself as ASEAN's crypto gateway.

**Hong Kong issues first stablecoin licenses to HSBC and Standard Chartered.** While not SEA proper, this has massive regional implications. Global stablecoin market cap: ~$309B as of January 2026. Singapore, UAE, and Japan already require full reserve backing and licensed issuers. The regulatory clarity cascade is making stablecoins legitimate financial infrastructure across Asia.

**BNM Malaysia's updated RMiT framework takes effect.** Bank Negara Malaysia's revised Risk Management in Technology guidelines (issued November 2025) now set minimum requirements for financial institutions managing technology risk, including cyber risk. This tightens the compliance bar for all fintech players in Malaysia.

## COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT

### Indonesia: The Great Reset

Indonesia's fintech lending market is experiencing its most significant structural shift since the sector emerged. What's happening:

- **Outstanding P2P lending:** Rp98.54T ($6B+) in January 2026, up 25.5% YoY
- **Industry profit:** Rp2.27T ($140M+) cumulative in 2025
- **But:** TWP90 delinquency at 4.33% (up from 2.52%), 24 operators above OJK's 5% threshold
- **Exits:** Maucash (Astra/WeLab), Pinjam Modal, Danakini, Ringan — these aren't fringe players
- **Survivors:** Those with balance sheet strength, governance, and underwriting discipline

The take: Indonesia still has a massive credit gap — 270M people, most underserved by traditional banks. The platforms that survive this shakeout will own the market. AdaKami alone contributed $411–649M to GDP through its loan disbursement multiplier effect. This is a market worth $6B+ in outstanding loans that's about to consolidate into fewer, stronger hands.

**For a new entrant:** The exit wave means potential acquisition targets are available at distressed valuations. Licenses from exiting players could be valuable.

### Philippines: Mobile-Total Economy

The Philippines is no longer "going digital" — it's there. Key signals:
- 57.4% of retail transactions are digital (exceeded BSP targets)
- GCash: 6M+ merchants, now offering international transfers, ADB-backed MSME lending
- Maya: all-in-one digital bank with wallet, payments, savings, credit, cards
- SharePHIL + GCash partnership for financial literacy and investor education
- 117% mobile penetration, 83.8% internet penetration

The super-app war between GCash and Maya is the most interesting competitive dynamic in SEA fintech right now. Both are building full financial stacks, but GCash's international expansion (16-country remittance) gives it a new dimension.

## TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. Stablecoin rails are eating correspondent banking.** TransFi's $19M raise, Insignia VC's analysis (6.49% fees → under 1%), and HSBC/StanChart getting stablecoin licenses all point the same direction. The Philippines' $40B remittance corridor is the prize — routing 10% through stablecoins saves $56M/year for Filipino workers alone. Watch for GCash or Maya to integrate stablecoin settlement within 12 months.

**2. Indonesia's fintech lending consolidation creates M&A opportunities.** With 22 lenders sanctioned and major players exiting, expect 2-3 significant acquisitions by mid-2026. Addentax Group (ATXG) is already in talks to acquire up to 20 APAC lending platforms. The surviving platforms will have pricing power.

**3. The "phygital" model is winning in emerging SEA.** GoTyme's 12M milestone proves that kiosk + app + retail partner distribution beats pure-digital in markets with trust gaps. Expect more digital banks to invest in physical touchpoints, especially in the Philippines, Indonesia, and Vietnam.

**4. AI-native compliance is becoming table stakes.** Insignia VC's analysis nails it: "In this region, compliance is not a feature. It is the product." With MAS freezing licenses and OJK sanctioning at scale, the fintechs that invest in AI-driven compliance and risk management will be the ones that survive the regulatory tightening cycle.

## OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Post-consolidation Indonesia lending plays.** With multiple OJK-licensed lenders exiting, there's a window to acquire distressed licenses and loan books at favorable valuations. Outstanding P2P lending is $6B+ and growing 25% YoY — the demand isn't going away, just the weak suppliers. A well-capitalized entrant with superior underwriting (AI-driven credit scoring using alternative data) could dominate the survivors' market.

**2. Philippines outbound remittance infrastructure.** GCash just launched international transfers, but the product is nascent (16 countries). Building stablecoin-powered remittance rails specifically for Philippines ↔ Middle East and Philippines ↔ ASEAN corridors could compress fees from 5-7% to under 1%. The $40B annual remittance market is still dominated by legacy providers.

**3. ASEAN SME cross-border payments.** The QR interoperability push (QRIS, PromptPay, PayNow, DuitNow) is solving consumer payments, but B2B cross-border for SMEs remains fragmented and expensive. Aspire ($44.7M revenue, 1772% growth) proves the demand exists. A payments platform focused on intra-ASEAN SME trade settlement — combining stablecoin rails with local e-wallet cash-out — could capture the gap between consumer QR payments and enterprise treasury.
