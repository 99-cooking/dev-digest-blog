# SEA Fintech Weekly — May 16–22, 2026

## TL;DR

Bank Indonesia dropped a surprise 50bps rate bomb to defend the rupiah, sending shockwaves through SEA's fintech lending ecosystem just as Indonesia's P2P NPLs were already climbing. Meanwhile, Thailand planted its flag as the region's crypto-forward regulator at Southeast Asia Blockchain Week, announcing Q3 Bitcoin and Ether ETFs. The week's biggest deal: Singapore's Aspire raised $158M Series B to double down on SME fintech across the region. And in the Philippines, Tonik became the first standalone digital bank to hit profitability — proving the lending-first model can work.

## NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**Tonik hits profitability — first standalone PH digital bank to do it.** Q1 2026 saw consolidated positive cash net income. The playbook: ignore user vanity metrics, focus on lending. Their $110M loan portfolio grew 2.3x YoY, with 99% of their $60M+ annualized revenue coming from loans. 82% loan-to-deposit ratio — highest among PH digital banks. AI-driven underwriting lets them profitably serve thin-file borrowers. This is the template everyone's watching. ([BackEndNews](https://backendnews.net/tonik-becomes-first-profitable-standalone-digital-bank/amp/))

**Tonik adjusts deposit rates downward.** Starting May 20: 5.50% (6mo), 6.00% (9mo), 6.50% (12mo), with a new ₱100K cap per time deposit. Classic profitable-bank move — they no longer need to buy deposits at premium rates. The rate compression signals confidence in their funding base.

**Philippine digital banks: profitability still rare.** Of six licensed operators (Tonik, UnionDigital, GoTyme, Maya Bank, UNO Digital Bank, OFBank), only Maya Bank and OFBank closed 2024 in the black (₱72.8M and ₱86.3M net income respectively). Tonik joining in Q1 2026 makes three. GoTyme and UnionDigital still burning through capital. ([CriptoTendencia](https://criptotendencia.com/2026/05/19/filipinas-los-bancos-digitales-crecen-a-ritmo-acelerado-pero-la-rentabilidad-sigue-siendo-su-gran-asignatura-pendiente/))

**Sea Group's Monee (fka SeaMoney) continues expanding.** Loan book exceeded $5B as of the rebrand. SeaBank operations across seven SEA markets + Taiwan. SPayLater, SLoan, and MariBank (Singapore) form the financial services backbone for Shopee's 600M+ user ecosystem. The question: can Monee decouple from Shopee's e-commerce cycle?

**Mantle launches UR — world's first onchain neobank with Swiss banking license.** Led by a former Ant Financial/Alipay exec who helped bring QR payments to SEA. UR targets Asia as its core growth market, aiming to do to digital fiat what QR codes did to cash. Tokenized deposits + DeFi integration. Early stage but worth watching — the team has actual SEA payments DNA. ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/boazsobrado/2026/05/15/from-alipay-to-defi-mantles-onchain-neobank-bets-on-asia/))

**99 App opens UK waitlist for Sharia-aligned neobank.** Not SEA-native, but relevant: ethical/Islamic digital banking is a $4T+ addressable market, and Malaysia and Indonesia are natural expansion targets. Watch this space.

## PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**QRIS goes live with China — the biggest cross-border QR deal yet.** Launched April 30, this connects Indonesia's QRIS with China's Alipay/UnionPay ecosystem. Users scan QR codes through domestic apps, pay in home currencies. Bank Indonesia Governor Perry Warjiyo and PBOC officials co-launched. This isn't just payments — it's Indonesia positioning QRIS as the ASEAN payment rail of choice. APEC-wide QRIS expansion announced (no date yet). ([Jakarta Globe](https://jakartaglobe.id/business/qris-officially-launches-in-china-enabling-cashless-crossborder-transactions), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3352360/china-indonesia-launch-cross-border-qr-payments-boost-global-yuan))

**BIS Project Nexus expected to go live in 2026.** The multilateral platform interlinking domestic fast payment systems (PromptPay, PayNow, QRIS, DuitNow) across ASEAN is on track. Bank Indonesia joined as a special observer in Feb 2026. When this goes live, real-time cross-border retail payments between ASEAN-5 become reality.

**ASEAN unified merchant database for cross-border transactions.** New initiative to create a shared merchant registry aligned with the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement. Goal: let MSMEs accept cross-border digital payments without bespoke integrations per country. ([Mindanao Daily News](https://mindanaodailynews.com/cross-border-transactions-unified-merchant-database-to-accelerate-msme-digital-adoption-strengthen-asean-economic-growth/))

**Atlantic Council: local-currency cross-border payments intensifying globally.** ASEAN explicitly highlighted as the most advanced region for QR-based local-currency settlement. The de-dollarization narrative in SEA payments is real and accelerating. ([Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/the-global-push-for-local-currency-cross-border-payments-is-intensifying/))

## REGULATORY & POLICY

**Bank Indonesia surprises with 50bps rate hike to 5.25%.** The first hike since April 2024 and double what markets expected. Driven by rupiah defense amid global dollar strength. Bloomberg: only 1 of 41 analysts predicted the full 50bps. This directly impacts fintech lending costs across Indonesia — expect margin compression for P2P lenders and BNPL players. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/bank-indonesia-surprises-markets-with-half-point-rate-hike), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/indonesia-central-bank-raises-interest-rates-by-more-than-expected-2026-05-20/))

**Philippines: BSP to take over online lending oversight from SEC.** FinTech Alliance.PH backs the move. The SEC and BSP are studying whether legislation is needed or if regulatory issuance suffices. This would put all digital lenders under central bank supervision — a significant tightening. Comes amid crackdowns on abusive online lending platforms. ([FinTech Alliance PH](https://fintechalliance.ph/fintech-group-backs-bsp-oversight-of-online-lenders/))

**OJK flags rising NPLs in Indonesia fintech lending.** P2P lending NPL ratio hit 4.52% by end of Q1 2026 — the regulator's "spotlight" issue. Despite this, total outstanding P2P lending grew 26.25% YoY to Rp 101.03 trillion ($6.3B). Profits for the industry actually fell, suggesting growth is coming at the expense of asset quality. The BI rate hike will make this worse.

**Bank Indonesia and Bank Negara Malaysia sign MoU.** Announced May 11 in Jakarta. Expands cooperation across monetary policy, financial stability, macroprudential oversight, and payment system development. Signals deeper Indonesia-Malaysia financial integration. ([OpenGov Asia](https://opengovasia.com/bank-indonesia-and-bank-negara-malaysia-sign-mou-to-strengthen-central-bank-cooperation/))

**BNM launches RM5B SME Stabilisation Relief Facility.** Open for applications from May 16 through Dec 31, 2026. Targeting MSMEs across Malaysia. Channeled through participating financial institutions — fintechs with lending licenses can potentially participate.

## COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT

### Indonesia — Under Pressure

Indonesia is the week's most consequential story. Three forces colliding:

1. **Monetary tightening:** BI's surprise 50bps hike to 5.25% is a rupiah defense play, but it raises borrowing costs across the board. Fintech lenders who depend on wholesale funding just got squeezed.

2. **Asset quality deterioration:** OJK data shows P2P NPLs at 4.52% and climbing. The industry grew 26% but profits fell. Classic late-cycle lending dynamics. The government is now building a unified platform (SAPA UMKM) to steer MSMEs toward formal lenders and away from illegal pinjol.

3. **Payment infrastructure ambition:** Despite macro headwinds, Indonesia is aggressively exporting QRIS. The China linkage went live April 30. APEC expansion announced. BI-BNM MoU signed. Indonesia wants QRIS to be the ASEAN standard — and they're executing.

**The tension:** Indonesia is simultaneously tightening monetary policy (bad for fintechs) while building the most ambitious payment infrastructure in ASEAN (great for payments fintechs). Winners: payment processors, merchant acquirers. Losers: undercapitalized P2P lenders.

### Thailand — The Crypto Play

Thailand used SEABW 2026 (May 20-21, Bangkok) to declare itself SEA's digital asset hub:

- **Spot BTC and ETH ETFs targeted for Q3 2026** — first in Southeast Asia
- Crypto futures market framework in development
- Security token sandbox programs with multiple financial institutions
- Real-world asset tokenization actively pursued
- SCB 10X (Siam Commercial Bank's venture arm) declared "the tokenized finance era has begun"

Thailand's SEC director Butree Vangsirirungruang called digital-asset support a "core strategy for the next three years." This is Hong Kong-level ambition applied to a $500B+ economy. If the ETFs launch on schedule, expect capital flows into Thai crypto infrastructure.

## TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. The lending squeeze is coming.** BI's rate hike + rising NPLs = consolidation in Indonesia's P2P lending space. Expect 2-3 smaller players to exit or merge by Q4. OJK will tighten further. Only well-capitalized, AI-driven underwriters (like what Tonik demonstrated in PH) survive.

**2. Stablecoins are the new rails.** StraitsX's XSGD is MAS-compliant. XUSD and XIDR launched. Circle's David Katz at SEABW said regulatory clarity is the catalyst. Asia dominates stablecoin payment volume (⅔ of global). The infrastructure layer is being built now — and SEA is leading.

**3. QR interoperability is ASEAN's killer app.** QRIS-China, Project Nexus, unified merchant databases — the pieces are clicking together. By end of 2026, a Thai tourist paying with PromptPay in Jakarta via QR will be unremarkable. This is the most consequential fintech development in the region.

**4. Thailand vs Singapore: the crypto hub race heats up.** Thailand's ETF push directly challenges Singapore's institutional-crypto lead. Singapore has regulation (MAS stablecoin framework) and infrastructure (StraitsX). Thailand has political will and market access. Vietnam's HCM City rising in fintech rankings adds a third contender.

## OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Indonesia MSME lending with embedded compliance.** The government is building SAPA UMKM — a platform to connect MSMEs with formal lenders. A fintech that integrates directly into this platform with AI underwriting and OJK-compliant reporting could capture the "formalization" wave as illegal pinjol get shut down. Rp 101T outstanding market, growing 26%/yr.

**2. Cross-border stablecoin settlement for SEA corridors.** StraitsX has XSGD/XUSD/XIDR. Nobody has stitched together a full SEA corridor (SGD→PHP, IDR→THB, etc.) using regulated stablecoins. The regulatory frameworks exist in Singapore and are emerging in Thailand. First mover on a multi-currency stablecoin remittance product wins the $60B+ SEA remittance market.

**3. Philippines deposit aggregation for digital banks.** Tonik proved lending-first works. But with six digital banks competing for deposits and rate compression happening, there's a gap for a deposit marketplace/aggregator that helps consumers optimize across Tonik, Maya, GoTyme, etc. Think Raisin for SEA. BSP's consumer protection push creates regulatory tailwind.
