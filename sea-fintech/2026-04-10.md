# SEA Fintech Weekly — April 4–10, 2026

## TL;DR

The biggest story this week is the rapid expansion of cross-border QR payment infrastructure across ASEAN, with South Korea-Indonesia going live, KBank-StraitsX launching stablecoin-settled Grab QR payments between Thailand and Singapore, and Vietnam connecting Alipay for Chinese tourists. Meanwhile, Grab dropped 13 new features at GrabX including Cash Loan (consumer lending) and GrabPay for Travel, signaling its most aggressive fintech push yet. The Global Payments Report 2026 confirmed what we've been watching: digital wallets have overtaken cards at POS in Singapore for the first time, and cash in Malaysia has collapsed from 64% to 22% of POS in six years.

## NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**Grab goes all-in on consumer lending with Cash Loan**
At its GrabX product showcase, Grab launched Cash Loan — consumer credit up to PHP 50,000 using behavioral data (ride frequency, GrabFood spend, platform tenure) as an alternative credit score. Live in Philippines, expanding to Thailand and Malaysia by mid-2026. Interest from 2.99%/month. This is Grab pivoting from merchant-partner lending to direct consumer credit — a significant expansion of its financial services footprint. ([Fintech News SG](https://fintechnews.sg/128715/fintech/grab-launches-fintech-tools-sea/))

**Only 15% of neobanks profitable globally in 2026**
FinTech Weekly's deep analysis argues neobanks "solved usability but didn't change the underlying architecture." The global neobank market is forecast to grow from $210B (2025) to $7.6T by 2034, but profitability remains elusive for 85% of players. The insight: real disruption requires an open earn layer, not just a prettier app. Relevant for SEA players like Tonik, GoTyme, and Maya still burning cash. ([FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/neobanks-disruption-open-financial-infrastructure-yield-2026))

**Philippines: GCash hits 94M users, but cash still king**
Global Payments Report 2026 data shows GCash now has 94 million users. Digital wallets capture 41% of PH e-commerce and 29% of POS. But cash still dominates at 42% in-store and 23% COD in e-commerce — the highest cash usage in the GPR 2026. ~50% of Filipinos remain unbanked. The gap between wallet downloads and actual daily usage remains the story. ([Fintech News SG](https://fintechnews.sg/128337/e-commerce/southeast-asia-payment-methods-2026-global-payments-report/))

**Revolut 70M+ users, tripling Singapore workforce**
Revolut now has over 70 million customers globally (March 2026). The company outlined plans to triple its Singapore workforce over three years, having already doubled it in 2025. Revolut's also opening a Paris HQ for western Europe, but the APAC build-out signals serious intent for the region. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolut), [FXC Intelligence](https://www.fxcintel.com/research/analysis/payment-infrastructure-asia-expansion))

**Wise gets full Thailand licensing**
Wise secured five licenses from the Bank of Thailand to issue foreign currency wallets and Wise-branded cards. This makes Thailand a full-service Wise market — cards, wallets, FX — and signals BoT's openness to foreign fintech entrants competing directly with local banks. ([FXC Intelligence](https://www.fxcintel.com/research/analysis/payment-infrastructure-asia-expansion))

**Airwallex Singapore revenue up 107% YoY**
After expanding into Indonesia, Vietnam, and South Korea in 2025, Airwallex plans to increase its APAC workforce by 70% by end of 2026. The company is emerging as a serious B2B payments infrastructure play in SEA, competing for the cross-border SME segment. ([FXC Intelligence](https://www.fxcintel.com/research/analysis/payment-infrastructure-asia-expansion))

## PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**Indonesia–South Korea cross-border QR payments go live**
Bank Indonesia and Bank of Korea officially linked their QR payment systems on April 1. Indonesian consumers can now pay in South Korea using QRIS via domestic apps — no FX conversion needed on the consumer side. This uses the Local Currency Transaction (LCT) framework, bypassing dollar intermediation. A landmark: QRIS now works in Japan, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, and South Korea. ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/south-korea-and-indonesia-roll-out-cross-border-qr-payment-service-in-local-currencies), [Asian Banking & Finance](https://asianbankingandfinance.net/cards-payments/news/indonesia-and-south-korea-link-qr-payments-bypass-foreign-exchange))

**KBank + StraitsX + Grab: stablecoin-powered cross-border payments**
This is the week's most technically interesting story. KasikornBank expanded Q Wallet so Thai travelers can scan GrabPay QR codes in Singapore. Under the hood: Q-money (KBank's e-money token) interoperates with XSGD (StraitsX's SGD stablecoin) for instant blockchain settlement. This is a live, production stablecoin cross-border payment between two major SEA economies, not a pilot. If this scales, it's a template for the entire region. ([Business Times SG](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/banking-finance/kasikornbank-enables-grab-qr-cross-border-payments-thai-travellers), [StraitsX Blog](https://www.straitsx.com/blog-post/kbank-expands-q-wallet-to-enable-thailand-singapore-cross-border-payments))

**Vietnam connects Alipay for Chinese tourists**
NAPAS, Vietcombank, and Ant International launched cross-border QR payments in Hanoi on April 3, enabling Chinese tourists to pay with Alipay at Vietnamese merchants. China is expected to join the broader ASEAN QR network next month. This is part of the de-dollarization trend: settlements in local currencies. ([Travel and Tour World](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/vietnam-joins-china-thailand-singapore-malaysia-kazakhstan-and-others-as-cross-border-qr-payments-simplify-travel-spending-and-trnsforming-it-into-digital-revolution-find-more-about-it-now/))

**Grab launches GrabPay for Travel & Tap to Pay**
GrabPay for Travel lets users scan local QR codes abroad and pay with linked cards — no e-wallet top-up needed. Rolling out in PH and MY (Q3), then SG and TH (year-end). Tap to Pay turns GrabMerchant phones into NFC terminals. Live in PH and SG, expanding region-wide. ([Fintech News SG](https://fintechnews.sg/128715/fintech/grab-launches-fintech-tools-sea/))

**QRIS heading to APEC**
Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs announced QRIS will be available across APEC member states, though no launch date was specified. Combined with 60M+ QRIS users domestically and 10M+ cross-border transactions between Indonesia-Malaysia alone in early 2026, Indonesia's payment infrastructure is becoming a regional export. ([Wikipedia/QRIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QRIS))

## REGULATORY & POLICY

**OJK sanctions 31 P2P lenders and 22 multifinance firms (March 2026)**
Indonesia's OJK handed out administrative sanctions to 31 fintech P2P lending companies and 22 multifinance firms during March. Separately, 10 P2P lenders haven't met minimum equity requirements, and 7 rural banks (BPR) had licenses revoked YTD. OJK is tightening the screws — the era of easy fintech licensing in Indonesia is over. P2P NPL ratio at 4.54% (still under the 5% threshold). ([Dunia Fintech](https://duniafintech.com/ojk-sanksi-22-multifinance-31-fintech-lending/), [Kontan](https://keuangan.kontan.co.id/news/ojk-catat-10-fintech-p2p-lending-belum-penuhi-ekuitas-minimum-per-februari-2026))

**BNM fines Bank Rakyat RM1M for IT security failures**
Malaysia's central bank penalized Bank Rakyat for non-compliance with Risk Management in Technology (RMiT) and Management of Customer Information and Permitted Disclosures (MCIPD) policy documents. Signal: BNM is enforcing digital security standards aggressively, relevant for fintechs operating under Malaysian banking partnerships. ([Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/04/07/bnm-fines-bank-rakyat-rm1m-for-it-security-failures/))

**Malaysia BNPL under Consumer Credit Act 2025**
BNPL balances hit RM4.9B ($1.5B) with 7.5M users and a 3.3% overdue ratio. BNM is moving providers toward licensing and responsible lending duties. The kicker: BNPL usage is concentrated among youth and lower-income users, primarily for essentials (food, groceries, transport) — it's becoming a cashflow coping mechanism, not a lifestyle product. ([Asian Banking & Finance](https://asianbankingandfinance.net/cards-payments/commentary/buy-now-worry-later-hidden-cost-bnpl))

**Vietnam names new central bank governor + crypto framework active**
Vietnam appointed a new SBV governor on April 8. The refinancing rate holds at 4.5% through 2026 despite inflation pressure. More significantly, Vietnam's crypto framework (Law on Digital Technology Industry) has been live since January 1, 2026 — officially recognizing digital assets as property. Vietnam went from gray zone to regulated market in one legislative cycle. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/vietnam-names-pham-duc-an-central-bank-governor-state-media-says-2026-04-08/), [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/vietnams-crypto-asset-regulation-framework-from-gray-zone-to-a-controlled-pilot-market/))

**Asia regulators tighten crypto exec accountability**
Across the region, MAS and peer regulators are requiring proof of competence and knowledge among key managerial staff for crypto license applications. This elevates compliance from "checkbox" to "board-level" requirement. ([CoinTurk](https://en.coin-turk.com/regulatory-changes-in-asia-drive-increased-accountability-for-crypto-industry-executives/))

## COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT

### Indonesia: The Payment Infrastructure Superpower

Indonesia dominated this week's headlines. Three major stories make the case that Indonesia is building the most ambitious payment infrastructure in SEA:

1. **QRIS–South Korea QR link goes live** — bypassing dollar intermediation entirely using LCT framework
2. **QRIS heading to APEC** — Indonesia is exporting its national payment standard to 21 economies
3. **60M+ QRIS users, 10M+ cross-border transactions** (Indonesia-Malaysia alone in early 2026)

Meanwhile, the regulatory side is tightening: OJK sanctioned 31 P2P lenders, revoked 7 bank licenses, and 10 fintechs haven't met equity minimums. Indonesia is simultaneously building world-class payment rails AND cleaning up its fintech ecosystem. The message: scale is welcome, but only with proper governance.

**P2P lending** hit Rp100.69 trillion ($6.1B) in cumulative disbursements through February 2026, up 25.75% YoY. NPL at 4.54%. Indonesia's fintech lending market is now genuinely large — and increasingly regulated like a real financial system.

**Crypto users** grew to 21.07 million (Feb 2026), up 1.76%, even as transaction volumes dipped. OJK is now considering primary market regulations to encourage local crypto projects.

### Vietnam: New Governor, New Crypto Framework, New Digital Push

Vietnam is in transition. New SBV governor Pham Duc An was appointed April 8, alongside a new finance minister. The country's crypto framework went live January 1, 2026. The SME Digital Transformation Plan 2026-2030 is a comprehensive infrastructure + fintech push. And the NAPAS-Alipay-Vietcombank cross-border QR launch shows Vietnam actively building payment connectivity with China.

## TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. Stablecoins are becoming real payment infrastructure, not just trading instruments.**
The KBank-StraitsX-Grab stablecoin settlement is the canary. Insignia's deep dive on local stablecoins ($1.2B supply, 80% used for payments not DeFi) confirms the thesis: local-currency stablecoins will power the next layer of SEA cross-border payments. Watch StraitsX (XSGD), and whoever builds XIDR, XPHP, XTHB.

**2. Grab is becoming a full-spectrum financial services company.**
Cash Loan + GrabPay for Travel + Tap to Pay + AI assistant in one product launch. Grab is layering lending, cross-border payments, and merchant acquiring on top of its ride-hail/food base. It now competes with banks, not just e-wallets.

**3. ASEAN QR interoperability is becoming the de facto cross-border payment standard for the region.**
Indonesia-South Korea live, Vietnam-China live, QRIS heading to APEC, Project Nexus coming. The bilateral spaghetti is messy but functional. By end of 2026, a tourist with a phone wallet from any major Asian country will be able to pay at any QR merchant in SEA. That's transformational.

**4. Regulatory divergence is accelerating.**
Vietnam legalized crypto assets. Malaysia is tightening BNPL. OJK is purging weak P2P lenders. Singapore is regulating stablecoins. Each country is finding its own path — there is no convergence toward a single SEA regulatory model.

## OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Local-currency stablecoin infrastructure**
StraitsX proved XSGD works in production with a major bank. There is no equivalent for IDR, PHP, THB, or VND at scale. The company that builds regulated, bank-connected stablecoins for these currencies — especially PHP (50% unbanked, massive remittance flows) and IDR (21M crypto users) — has a massive TAM. The regulatory window is opening, not closing.

**2. "Cashflow stress" fintech for young SEA consumers**
Malaysia's BNPL data reveals the real underserved need: youth and low-income users borrowing for essentials. The opportunity isn't more BNPL — it's income smoothing, micro-savings, earned wage access, and financial wellness tools built for the 70% of SEA consumers who live paycheck-to-paycheck. Nobody owns this category yet.

**3. Cross-border merchant payment aggregation**
With QRIS, DuitNow, PromptPay, PayNow, and GrabPay all becoming interoperable, the merchant who sells to tourists needs a single integration point. The aggregator that unifies all these QR schemes into one merchant dashboard — with multi-currency settlement, real-time reporting, and regulatory compliance across 6 markets — is a $1B company waiting to be built.
