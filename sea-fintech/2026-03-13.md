# SEA Fintech Weekly — Mar 7–13, 2026

## 🎯 TL;DR

Thailand is quietly becoming ASEAN's digital payments superpower — an IMF report crowned it the region's leader with 350 PromptPay transactions per person per year, while it formally joined the five-nation cross-border QR payment network alongside Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines, and Vietnam. Meanwhile, Indonesia's new OJK board chair Friderica Widyasari Dewi took the reins with a digital asset regulation mandate, the Philippines' "eBayad Act" cleared its second reading to mandate digital government payments, and Mastercard launched a Crypto Partner Program with 85+ firms (Circle, Binance, Ripple) — signaling that stablecoin rails are going mainstream faster than anyone expected.

## 🏦 NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**Sea Group's Monee fintech booms, but stock craters 50%** — Sea's fintech arm posted Q4 revenue of US$1.1B (+54.3% YoY) with consumer/SME loans hitting US$9.2B outstanding (+80.4% YoY). Yet the stock is down 50% in six months as e-commerce losses from the Shopee vs TikTok Shop/Lazada war drag margins. Sea injected another S$75M into Maribank (Singapore digital bank), bringing total paid-up capital to ~US$639M. The disconnect between Monee's performance and Sea's share price is the most interesting story in SEA fintech right now — fintech alone can't save a super-app at war.

**Tonik eyes Tokyo Stock Exchange via Mizuho partnership** — In a notable trend piece, Insignia Ventures revealed that SEA startups are increasingly eyeing TSE listings over traditional Wall Street IPOs. Tonik (Philippines digital bank) secured a strategic Series B from Mizuho Bank, positioning for a potential TSE listing. The "Tokyo Pathway" now has 20+ companies in TSE's Asia Startup Hub — Japanese corporates get growth engines, SEA startups get stable capital. Watch for GoTyme, Fazz, and others to follow.

**GoTyme Bank rebrands in South Africa** — TymeBank (South Africa) rebranded to GoTyme Bank in January 2026, aligning with its Philippines branding. The Tyme Group's dual-market strategy (PH + SA) is increasingly coherent — both markets serve large unbanked populations with mobile-first banking.

**India's neobank Fi shuts down banking services** — A cautionary tale: Fi, founded by ex-Google Pay executives, is winding down banking services after 4+ years. Reminder that neobank unit economics without lending or payments moats remain brutal. SEA neobanks should take note.

**EBANX moves global HQ to Singapore** — Brazilian payments giant EBANX (48% annual TPV growth) officially established its global HQ in Singapore after securing an MPI license from MAS. Singapore continues to cement its position as the region's fintech command center.

## 💸 PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**Thailand joins 5-nation ASEAN cross-border QR payment network** — Thailand formally joined Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines, and Vietnam in a cross-border QR payment system. Tourists can now pay via mobile apps and QR codes across all five countries without currency exchange. This is the real-world realization of ASEAN payment interoperability — and it's being driven by tourism economics. PromptPay-PayNow, QRIS-DuitNow, and InstaPay linkages are now functional.

**Thailand crowned ASEAN's digital payment leader (IMF)** — A new IMF report confirmed Thailand leads ASEAN in cashless adoption: 350 PromptPay transactions/person/year (up 8x since 2019), 90M+ registrations vs 71M population, 74M+ daily transactions, and ~50% e-money account penetration (vs ~20% ASEAN average). 80% of Thai adults now own financial accounts.

**Moolahgo x LianLian Global: Singapore-China cross-border payments** — MAS-licensed Moolahgo partnered with China's LianLian Global to expand cross-border payment services for ASEAN SMEs. They're also running QRIS promotions (win flights to Bali) — creative adoption-driving tactics.

**Visa x Trip.com virtual travel card for Asia Pacific** — Visa and Trip.com launched a global virtual travel card program (announced Mar 11) designed to simplify travel payments across APAC. Another signal that embedded travel-finance is a growing wedge.

**Wizz Financial: first stablecoin cross-border transaction US→80 countries** — Wizz Financial (India/SEA/GCC corridor focus) completed its first stablecoin-powered remittance via BitGo. The US-to-emerging-markets stablecoin corridor is now live.

## 📋 REGULATORY & POLICY

**Indonesia: New OJK board appointed, digital asset regulation prioritized** — Friderica Widyasari Dewi was appointed OJK Chair for 2026-2031 after mass resignations rocked the previous board. New executive Adi Budiarso will lead the Financial Sector Technology Innovation and Digital Assets (ITSK-IAKD) division. Eight strategic priorities include strengthening consumer protection and advancing digital asset regulation. This is the most consequential regulatory leadership change in SEA fintech this year.

**Philippines: "eBayad Act" passes second reading** — House Bill 8468 cleared its second reading, mandating digital payments for all government transactions nationwide. If enacted, this would force digital payment infrastructure into every LGU and government office — a massive demand-side push for GCash, Maya, and the entire PH fintech ecosystem.

**Philippines: BSP sets June 30 deadline to move beyond SMS OTPs** — Banks must strengthen authentication and reduce SMS OTP reliance by June 30, 2026. This pushes banks toward biometrics, in-app authentication, and passkeys — creating opportunities for auth-tech providers.

**Malaysia: XTransfer gets conditional BNM payment license** — China's XTransfer received conditional approval from Bank Negara Malaysia for key payment licenses, marking its formal entry into the Malaysian market for B2B cross-border payments.

**Malaysia: BNM/SC invite feedback on sustainable finance taxonomy** — Deadline April 14, 2026 for comments on the proposed Malaysia Taxonomy structure. ESG-fintech intersection gaining regulatory attention.

**Vietnam: SBV declares 2026 the "year of accelerating" digital transformation** — After establishing legal frameworks and data infrastructure in 2025, Vietnam's banking sector is going full throttle on digital. The SBV is also proposing to quadruple small loan limits (from 100M VND) and giving banks more autonomy in setting limits.

**ASEAN DEFA negotiations advance in Manila** — Negotiators and legal experts met in Manila to push forward the Digital Economy Framework Agreement, covering digital trade, SME support, and cross-border integration. The regulatory infrastructure for a unified ASEAN digital market is slowly materializing.

## 🌏 COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT

### Thailand: The Quiet Payments Superpower

Thailand is having a moment. The IMF officially ranked it ASEAN's digital payment leader, and the numbers are staggering:

- **PromptPay:** 90M+ registrations (71M population), 74M+ daily transactions, 350 txns/person/year
- **E-money:** ~50% adult penetration vs ~20% ASEAN average
- **Financial inclusion:** 80% of adults hold financial accounts, 107M registered mobile banking accounts
- **Cross-border:** Now live in the 5-nation ASEAN QR payment network
- **Money20/20 Asia:** Coming to Bangkok April 21-23, signaling Thailand's centrality to the APAC fintech narrative

Bangkok Bank's attempt to impose a ฿2,000 minimum balance on e-banking accounts (to curb mule accounts) was reversed after massive backlash — 90% of accounts hold less than ฿2,000. This shows the tension between fraud prevention and financial inclusion in a deeply digital market.

### Philippines: Legislative Momentum

The Philippines is moving on two regulatory fronts simultaneously. The "eBayad Act" (HB 8468) would mandate digital payments in all government transactions — a game-changer for adoption. Meanwhile, BSP's June 30 deadline to eliminate SMS OTP dependency forces banks to modernize authentication infrastructure. Combined with Salmon's embedded lending expansion and the active digital bank competition (Maya, Tonik, GoTyme), PH remains the most dynamic market for fintech disruption in SEA.

## 🔮 TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. Stablecoin rails going mainstream, fast.** Mastercard's 85-firm Crypto Partner Program (Circle, Binance, Ripple) isn't a crypto project — it's payments infrastructure. Wizz Financial's live stablecoin remittances, Kast's $80M raise, and the US GENIUS Act are converging. SEA remittance corridors (Philippines OFW, Indonesia TKI) are prime targets for stablecoin disruption in 2026.

**2. The Tokyo IPO pathway is real.** SEA fintechs are pivoting from Wall Street to TSE listings — Tonik/Mizuho is the template. Japanese capital + SEA growth = a structural arbitrage that 20+ companies are now pursuing. This changes the exit calculus for VCs backing SEA fintech.

**3. ASEAN QR interoperability is creating a single payment market.** Five nations live on cross-border QR. When Indonesia formally joins (QRIS linkage is being piloted), it will cover ~600M people. This is quietly the most important fintech infrastructure story in the world right now.

**4. AI agents are coming for fintech.** Money20/20 Asia's preview report highlights AI and digital assets as the twin themes. CB Insights predicts "Know Your Agent" (KYA) tooling will become as foundational as KYC. The agent-driven finance era is beginning — SEA's high mobile penetration makes it a natural testing ground.

## 💡 OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Government payment infrastructure (Philippines)** — The eBayad Act will mandate digital payments in all government transactions. There's a massive B2G opportunity in providing payment rails, settlement systems, and training for local government units. Think: the Stripe of Philippine government payments.

**2. Embedded motorcycle/vehicle financing across SEA** — Salmon's POS lending model in PH dealerships is replicable across Indonesia (world's 3rd largest motorcycle market), Vietnam, and Thailand. The 2-wheeler financing market in SEA is >$20B and still dominated by traditional finance companies with 3-day approval cycles. Instant, in-store, digital-first lending is wide open.

**3. Stablecoin-powered OFW/TKI remittance** — The Philippines receives ~$36B/year in remittances, Indonesia ~$12B. Current corridors charge 5-8%. Stablecoin rails (now legitimized by Mastercard, Wizz, and regulatory tailwinds) can cut this to <1%. The first SEA-native stablecoin remittance app with local cash-out partnerships wins a massive market.
