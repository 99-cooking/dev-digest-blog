# SEA Fintech Weekly — April 18–24, 2026

## 🎯 TL;DR

Money20/20 Asia took over Bangkok this week, and the consensus was loud: stablecoins are infrastructure now, not speculation. Meanwhile, Salmon Group closed a $100M round to attack the Philippines, WeChat Pay quietly plugged into domestic QR systems across five Asian countries (including three SEA markets), and Sea Limited bet big on AI with a Singapore Centre of Excellence. The theme of the week: the plumbing of SEA finance is being rebuilt in real time — by fintechs, not banks.

---

## 🏦 NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**Maya wins "Neobank of the Year" at Triple A Digital Awards.** The Philippines' Maya Bank took home the top neobank prize alongside Best Digital Fraud Protection Experience at The Asset Triple A Digital Awards 2026. It's a vindication of Maya's strategy: build trust through security first, scale second. The fraud protection win is particularly telling — in a market where scam fatigue is real, Maya is leaning into safety as a differentiator.

**Sea Limited establishes AI Centre of Excellence in Singapore.** Sea announced an AI CoE backed by Digital Industry Singapore, creating 100+ R&D roles over three years. The headline: their in-house LLM, Compass Max v3.5 (245B parameters), is already live across Shopee and tailored for SEA languages and e-commerce. This matters for SeaMoney — expect AI-native lending and fraud detection features to flow from this investment. Sea is positioning itself as the only SEA super-app that builds its own foundation models.

**Touch 'n Go's 24M users make banks nervous in Malaysia.** A viral clip of a bank executive stating that traditional lenders should worry more about TNG eWallet than digital banks sparked national debate. At 24M+ users, TNG's base exceeds most traditional Malaysian banks. The implication: the real competitive threat to incumbents isn't neobanks with banking licenses — it's e-wallets that already own the customer relationship and are adding financial services on top.

**Salmon Group raises $100M for Philippines expansion.** The dual-tranche deal ($60M equity + $40M Nordic bonds at 13.7% yield) was significantly oversubscribed. Salmon operates through a BSP-licensed bank and SEC-licensed financing company. Their app sits at 4.8 stars with 99.9% uptime, offering 8% term deposits and 62-day grace period credit lines. Investors: Spice Expeditions (lead), WUIMC, Moore Strategic Ventures, FJ Labs. This is one of the largest SEA fintech rounds in Q2.

**EY report: SEA financial services deal volume up, but values down.** 58 disclosed deals worth US$2.1B in 2025 (vs. 48 deals / $4.2B in 2024). Indonesia is turning "more selective" — investors favor smaller, capability-driven acquisitions over mega-deals. Digital banking, payments, and fintech are the main drivers. Translation: the easy-money era is over, but smart capital is still flowing to proven models.

**Fintech founders at Money20/20: "There is no shortcut."** DeeMoney, Aspire, and HitPay founders laid bare the unglamorous reality of building in SEA — fragmented regulation, multi-market licensing nightmares, and the limits of AI when your core product isn't solid. Aspire (operating in ~8 jurisdictions) says multi-market presence is now its moat, not its burden. HitPay deliberately chose licenses-first, growth-second — and it's paying off in year ten.

## 💸 PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**WeChat Pay integrates with domestic QR systems across 5 Asian markets.** Chinese tourists can now scan DuitNow QR (Malaysia), PromptPay (Thailand), and SGQR+ (Singapore) directly through WeChat Pay — no app switching, no currency handling. This quietly makes WeChat Pay the most interoperable cross-border wallet in Asia. For SEA merchants, it's a direct line to Chinese spending power without any integration work.

**EBANX expands into Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Vietnam.** The Brazilian cross-border payments giant is making a major push into SEA, targeting markets where "traditional banking falls short of supporting digital commerce." EBANX specializes in connecting global merchants with local payment methods — a play on the fact that credit card penetration remains low across the region. This puts them in direct competition with local acquirers and payment aggregators.

**StraitsX connects KBank's Q Wallet with GrabPay QR via stablecoin settlement.** Covered in Insignia VC's analysis of agentic commerce: travelers pay through familiar wallets, merchants receive local currency, and XSGD-linked blockchain settlement handles the complexity invisibly. This is stablecoin infrastructure doing what it was always supposed to do — plumbing, not product.

**PromptPay now linked to 5 countries + Japan.** Thailand's instant payment system has become the backbone of ASEAN payment interoperability, with live connections to Singapore (PayNow), Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, and Japan. Over 90M registrations and 74M daily transactions. PromptPay is quietly becoming the de facto standard other countries build toward.

**GCash blocks 4,900 "quishing" scam merchants.** The Philippine e-wallet is fighting a rising tide of QR phishing attacks — scammers create fake QR codes that mimic GCash payment pages. The company took down suspicious accounts and is working with government agencies. As QR adoption accelerates across SEA, QR-based fraud is becoming the new frontier. Every wallet operator needs a quishing strategy.

## 📋 REGULATORY & POLICY

**Money20/20 Policy20: Asia's regulators call for "Sovereign Intelligence."** Over 80 policymakers, regulators, and central bank governors convened in Bangkok under Chatham House Rule. The key theme: co-creation between regulators and industry as finance enters an era where AI and stablecoins demand new frameworks. The "Sovereign Intelligence" framing suggests regulators are thinking about how to maintain monetary sovereignty in a world of borderless digital money.

**HKMA issues stablecoin licenses to HSBC and Standard Chartered (April 2026).** While technically Hong Kong rather than SEA, this has massive implications for the region. Major global banks now have permission to issue stablecoins — expect these to flow into SEA trade corridors quickly. Singapore's MAS will be watching closely.

**BSP sandbox enabling fintech experimentation in the Philippines.** The Philippines' BSP Circular No. 9 (Series 2024) regulatory sandbox continues to mature, allowing fintechs to pilot digital assets, tokenized instruments, and cross-border trading platforms in a supervised environment. This is how the Philippines plans to stay competitive against Singapore's more established regulatory frameworks.

**SEON reports strong Indonesia momentum under OJK compliance.** The fraud-prevention firm expanded its Indonesian customer base across digital banking, lending, and payments, supporting OJK compliance requirements. Indonesia's regulatory environment is tightening — fintechs that can't demonstrate robust fraud/AML compliance will be squeezed out.

## 🔮 TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. Stablecoins as invisible infrastructure.** The Money20/20 consensus was clear: stablecoins are no longer retail products — they're settlement rails. The StraitsX/KBank/GrabPay integration and HKMA licensing of HSBC/StanChart for stablecoin issuance are the opening moves. By year-end, expect at least one major SEA corridor (likely SG-TH or SG-PH) to have stablecoin-settled real-time payments running at meaningful volume.

**2. Agentic commerce is coming to SEA.** Insignia VC's deep analysis of AI agents doing shopping/procurement is not hypothetical — 69K agents on x402 have already processed $50M. SEA's fragmented payment landscape actually makes this harder (many local rails) but the reward is bigger. First movers building agent-compatible payment APIs will have a compounding advantage.

**3. E-wallets are the new banks.** Touch 'n Go in Malaysia, GCash in Philippines — these aren't payment apps anymore, they're financial platforms with larger customer bases than most banks. The strategic question for every SEA market: will regulators let e-wallets become banks, or will they protect incumbents? Malaysia's debate this week suggests the tension is reaching a breaking point.

**4. QR interoperability is accelerating faster than anyone expected.** WeChat Pay plugging into DuitNow, PromptPay, and SGQR+ in one move. Korea-India QR payments launching this year. PromptPay linked to 6 countries. The dream of a pan-ASEAN payment network is materializing — not through a single system, but through bilateral bridges that compound into a mesh.

## 💡 OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Anti-fraud-as-a-service for SEA e-wallets.** GCash blocking 4,900 quishing merchants is just the tip of the iceberg. As QR payments scale across the region, every wallet and merchant acquirer needs sophisticated fraud detection. SEON's Indonesia expansion proves the demand. A SEA-native anti-fraud layer, built with local scam pattern intelligence, could be a $100M+ business.

**2. Cross-border SME treasury for ASEAN.** EBANX's SEA expansion and Aspire's multi-jurisdiction play both point to the same gap: SMEs selling across borders have terrible financial infrastructure. A neobank that combines multi-currency accounts, cross-border QR acceptance, and AI-powered FX management for ASEAN SMEs would be entering a market with near-zero competition and massive tailwinds from QR interoperability.

**3. AI-native credit scoring for the newly banked.** Salmon and Maya are both acquiring millions of customers who have thin or no credit files. Sea's 245B-parameter SEA-native LLM shows the AI capability exists locally. An AI credit scoring engine trained on SEA-specific alternative data (e-wallet transaction patterns, telco data, e-commerce behavior) — sold B2B to neobanks and fintechs — is an infrastructure play waiting to happen.
