# SEA Fintech Weekly — March 21–27, 2026

## TL;DR

Singapore's MAS BLOOM sandbox made headlines as Ripple, DBS, OCBC, Stripe, Circle, JP Morgan, and Coinbase began testing stablecoin-powered trade settlement — the most significant institutional stablecoin pilot in SEA to date. Meanwhile, Grab posted record $3.37B revenue for 2025 and is guiding $4.04–4.10B for 2026, while expanding aggressively into personal lending in Thailand. Wise became the first non-bank to receive a full payment license set in Thailand, launching PromptPay integration on May 19. Indonesia is pushing QRIS interoperability across all APEC economies, having already processed $105M in cross-border QR payments with Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand.

## NEOBANK & DIGITAL BANKING

**MariBank (Sea Group) opens public access + launches $1 equity investing — Singapore**
Sea Group's digital bank MariBank will offer full public access through its standalone app starting April 2026, ending a long invite-only phase. It's simultaneously launching a Singapore equities investment product with a $1 minimum — the lowest entry point of any bank in the country. MariBank also waived its 3% foreign transaction fee from January 2026. This signals Sea Group is finally getting serious about competing with GXS (Grab-Singtel) beyond deposits.
[Source: Asian Banking & Finance](https://asianbankingandfinance.net/news/maribank-rolls-out-s1-equity-fund-invite-only-phase)

**GoTyme eyes profitability by mid-2027 — Philippines**
GoTyme Bank, the Gokongwei-Tyme JV in the Philippines, disclosed it's targeting profitability by mid-2027. The Philippines now has six licensed digital banks: GoTyme, UNO, Tonik, UnionDigital, OFBank, and Maya Bank. The race to profitability is intensifying — whoever gets there first sets the narrative for BSP's evaluation of whether digital bank licenses were worth issuing.
[Source: Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/03/23/business/corporate-news/gotyme-profitability-to-be-hit-by-mid-2027/2305044)

**Simon-Kucher: Neobanks hit 1.4 billion accounts globally**
The "Neobanking: Beyond Disruption" report finds neobanks are no longer niche — they're increasingly becoming primary banking relationships. SEA is singled out as a key growth theater. The global figure of 850M neobank users underscores how fast challenger banks are scaling.
[Source: Yahoo Finance SG](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/simon-kucher-report-finds-neobanks-110100849.html)

**GCash kills SMS OTPs — Philippines**
GCash stopped sending OTPs via SMS on March 24, switching entirely to in-app push notifications. This aligns with BSP's push to phase out SMS-based authentication (a major fraud vector in PH). Maya is expected to follow. The move is significant — GCash has ~90M registered users, making this one of the largest OTP migration events in SEA.
[Source: YugaTech](https://www.yugatech.com/news/gcash-to-stop-sms-otps-starting-march-24/)

**BSP orders password phase-out for fintechs — Philippines**
Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas is pushing fintech firms to eliminate password-based authentication. Maya and other digital banks are expected to comply in coming months, moving toward biometric and passkey-based login. The Philippines is quietly becoming one of SEA's most aggressive regulators on digital security.
[Source: Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/03/26/business/top-business/fintech-firms-start-password-phaseout/2307522)

**Treasure Global's ZCITY sees 7x ARPU growth — Malaysia**
Treasure Global, operator of the ZCITY fintech platform in Malaysia, reported 7x ARPU growth. Its CEO received the ASEAN Fintech Icon Award at the ASEAN Banking & Finance Summit 2026. Small player, but the ARPU trajectory signals Malaysian consumer willingness to deepen engagement with fintech super-apps.
[Source: GlobeNewsWire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/26/3263012/0/en/)

## PAYMENTS & REMITTANCE

**Indonesia expands QRIS across all APEC economies**
Bank Indonesia announced plans to extend QRIS interoperability to all APEC member states. Current cross-border corridors with Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand already processed IDR 1.66 trillion ($105M) by mid-2025. Malaysia is the largest corridor: 3.4M transactions from MY→ID in Jan–Sep 2025 alone. QRIS now has 39.3M merchants and 57M users domestically, processing 6.05B transactions worth $37B in H1 2025. This is the most ambitious QR interoperability push in the world.
[Source: ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/indonesias-qris-expansion-across-apec-and-what-it-means-for-businesses/)

**Wise gets first non-bank full license in Thailand**
Wise received full payment service licenses from BOT, becoming the first non-bank to offer a multi-currency wallet in Thailand. PromptPay integration launches May 19, allowing Thai-based users to send baht internationally and receive via PromptPay. This is a milestone for Thailand's payments market — the regulatory wall that protected domestic banks just got a crack.
[Source: TechNode Global](https://technode.global/2026/03/18/wise-gets-approval-to-offer-payment-services-in-thailand/)

**Fiuu processes $13B in payments across SEA in 2025**
Malaysia-headquartered Fiuu (formerly MOLPay/Razer Merchant Services) hit $13B in TPV for 2025, up 32.65% YoY, across 960M+ transactions. In 2026, they're expanding Mastercard Click-to-Pay, Visa installments, JCB direct acquiring, and WeChat Pay across MY/SG/PH. They were also first to enable Samsung Pay Online in Malaysia. Fiuu is quietly becoming SEA's most connected payment gateway.
[Source: LionhearTV](https://www.lionheartv.net/2026/03/fiuu-processed-us13-billion-in-total-payment-volume-in-2025-up-32-65-from-2024/)

**Project Nexus: BIS pushes multilateral payment linkage**
Forbes examined how close SEA is to a true regional payments network. The consensus: bilateral QR linkages (Thailand-Indonesia, Singapore-Malaysia, etc.) are useful but don't scale. Project Nexus, the BIS-backed multilateral platform connecting domestic fast payment systems, is expected to go live in 2026. If successful, it would make bilateral agreements redundant.
[Source: Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/zennonkapron/2026/03/22/how-close-is-southeast-asia-to-building-a-regional-payments-network/)

**Maya launches remittance rebates for Middle East OFWs — Philippines**
Maya rolled out a limited-time rebate program (March 23–April 10) targeting Filipino families receiving remittances from the Middle East, including Bahrain, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, UAE, and others. Context: the US-Israel conflict with Iran is impacting OFWs in the region. Smart move — remittance is Maya's core wedge against GCash.
[Source: BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/the-nation/2026/03/24/738504/)

## REGULATORY & POLICY

**MAS BLOOM sandbox: Stablecoins meet institutional finance — Singapore**
The Monetary Authority of Singapore's BLOOM initiative is now the most important stablecoin sandbox globally. Ripple joined on March 25 to pilot RLUSD for cross-border trade settlement, partnering with supply-chain fintech Unloq. Other participants: DBS, OCBC, JP Morgan, Stripe, Circle, and Coinbase. MAS also signed an MoU with Deutsche Bundesbank on cross-border digital asset settlements and is preparing a 2026 pilot for tokenized government bills settled in wholesale CBDC.
[Source: CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/25/ripple-taps-singapore-s-central-bank-sandbox-to-test-stablecoin-powered-trade-finance-with-rlusd)

**OJK extends P2P lending moratorium — Indonesia**
OJK confirmed the moratorium on new P2P lending licenses will continue through 2026. Only 95 licensed fintech lenders remain. Meanwhile, OJK is reviewing the license of Dana Syariah Indonesia (sharia-compliant P2P). The signal: Indonesia wants existing players to consolidate and improve credit quality before issuing new licenses.
[Source: Kontan](https://newssetup.kontan.co.id/news/ojk-masih-moratorium-izin-fintech-cek-daftar-pinjol-legal-agar-tak-tertipu-ilegal)

**OJK gets new leadership — Indonesia**
AFTECH (Indonesia's fintech association) noted OJK's new leadership formation as a moment to strengthen governance across the fintech ecosystem. Digital payment transaction volume hit 4.67B transactions through February 2026, up 40.35% YoY.
[Source: Teropong News](https://teropongnews.com/2026/03/aftech-nilai-formasi-baru-ojk-momentum-penguatan-governance-ekosistem-fintech)

**Thailand drafts AI legislation for fintech — BOT/ETDA**
Thailand's Electronic Transactions Development Agency is finalizing draft AI legislation based on the EU's risk-based framework, expected by late 2026. Medium-risk applications — including credit assessment and lending — will require transparency and audit controls. This will directly impact how Grab, True Money, and other platforms use AI for underwriting.
[Source: Acclime Thailand](https://thailand.acclime.com/news/regional-ai-data-centre-hub/)

**Malaysia overhauls hire purchase financing — BNM**
Starting June 2026, Malaysian banks must overhaul hire purchase financing with clearer terms and disclosures. BNM continues its pattern of incremental but meaningful consumer protection reforms.
[Source: Fintech News MY](https://fintechnews.my/)

## COUNTRY SPOTLIGHT

### Singapore — The Stablecoin Capital

Singapore is making an unmistakable bid to become the global hub for regulated stablecoin infrastructure. This week alone:

- **BLOOM sandbox** brought together Ripple, DBS, OCBC, JP Morgan, Stripe, Circle, and Coinbase to test stablecoin-settled trade finance
- **MAS–Bundesbank MoU** on cross-border digital asset settlements
- **Wholesale CBDC pilot** for tokenized government bills coming in 2026
- **MariBank** (Sea Group) going fully public in April with $1 equity investing
- **Hong Kong stablecoin licenses** expected in March 2026 create competitive pressure

Singapore's strategy is clear: let private stablecoins and tokenized assets coexist under heavy regulation, use BLOOM as the proving ground, and position the city-state as the settlement layer for Asian trade finance. The participant list (mixing TradFi heavyweights with crypto-native firms) signals MAS wants to own the middle ground between DeFi and CeFi.

### Indonesia — The Volume King

Indonesia's fintech numbers are staggering:
- **QRIS:** 39.3M merchants, 57M users, 6.05B transactions, $37B volume in H1 2025
- **P2P lending:** IDR 98.54T outstanding, growing 25.52% YoY
- **Digital payments:** 4.67B transactions through Feb 2026, up 40.35% YoY
- **Cross-border QR:** Now live with MY, SG, TH — expanding to all APEC

OJK's moratorium on new P2P licenses shows maturity: the regulator is prioritizing quality over quantity. The QRIS-to-APEC push is Indonesia's most ambitious payments diplomacy yet — if it succeeds, QRIS becomes the de facto QR standard for the Asia-Pacific.

## TRENDS & PREDICTIONS

**1. Stablecoin infrastructure is the new battleground**
Singapore's BLOOM, Hong Kong's upcoming stablecoin licenses, and Tether's increased SEA investments signal that regulated stablecoins — not CBDCs — may become the dominant cross-border settlement mechanism in Asia. Watch for MAS to announce BLOOM results by Q3 2026.

**2. Superapps are becoming financial conglomerates**
Grab's Stash acquisition + Thailand personal lending + record revenue growth. GoTo's Bank Jago partnership. Sea Group's MariBank going public. The superapp-to-bank pipeline is maturing. The question is no longer "will they do banking?" but "will regulators let them become systemically important?"

**3. SEA payments are converging toward pull/recurring models**
Quona Capital's analysis comparing Brazil's Pix ecosystem to SEA's QR rails is prescient. SEA's push-payment QR codes are mature; the next frontier is pull payments (recurring subscriptions, loan repayments, insurance premiums). Whoever builds the "Pix Automático" equivalent for QRIS/PromptPay/QRPH first wins embedded finance.

**4. AI regulation will shape credit access**
Thailand's draft AI legislation (risk-based, EU-modeled) will set the tone for how SEA regulates AI in lending. If medium-risk AI applications require audit trails, it could slow down data-driven lending products like Grab Quick Cash — or it could create a moat for well-resourced players who can afford compliance.

## OPPORTUNITY RADAR

**1. Securitization infrastructure for SEA fintech lenders**
Quona Capital's Brazil-to-SEA analysis identifies the single biggest structural gap: SEA lacks a securitization framework like Brazil's FIDC. Fintech lenders are capital-constrained because there's no standardized way to package and sell receivables to institutional investors. Building the "Kanastra of Southeast Asia" — an end-to-end back-office for securitizing fintech loan portfolios — could unlock billions in latent liquidity.

**2. Recurring/pull payment layer on top of QR rails**
QRIS, QRPH, PromptPay, and VietQR are all push-payment systems. Nobody has built a cross-platform recurring payment layer that works across SEA's fragmented QR infrastructure. The company that enables subscription billing, loan repayment, and insurance premium collection via QR mandates will own embedded finance distribution.

**3. OFW-focused neobank with Middle East corridor**
Maya's remittance rebate program reveals an underserved niche: Filipino families dependent on Middle East remittances. A purpose-built neobank combining remittance (competitive FX), savings (high-yield deposits), and insurance (OFW coverage) for the 2.2M+ Filipino workers in the Gulf could capture meaningful market share from GCash and Maya's generic offerings.
