## 🤖 AI & AGENTS

**Anthropic Closing $30-50B Round at ~$900-950B Valuation**
Bloomberg and NYT both reported Anthropic is in late-stage talks to raise $30-50B at a valuation up to $950B — surpassing OpenAI's $852B March valuation for the first time. Co-led by Sequoia, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Altimeter. Expected to close end of May. **Why it matters:** This isn't growth funding — it's infrastructure-scale capital for AWS and Google Cloud compute commitments through 2027. Anthropic went from $380B in February to potentially $950B in three months. The message: whoever controls compute controls the frontier. Claude Mythos Preview's 94.6% GPQA score gave them the technical credibility to demand this kind of valuation. **Watch:** Whether the round closes before Google I/O (tomorrow, May 19) potentially reshapes competitive dynamics with Gemini 4.0.

**OpenAI Launches $4B "Deployment Company" — Acquires Tomoro**
OpenAI spun up a separate consulting entity backed by $4B from TPG, Advent, Bain Capital, and Brookfield. First move: acquiring AI consulting firm Tomoro for its 150 forward-deployed engineers. The unit will embed OpenAI engineers directly into enterprise clients. **Why it matters:** OpenAI is becoming Accenture. This is the clearest signal yet that the model business alone won't justify the valuation — they need services revenue. It's also a defensive play: if enterprises can't deploy AI successfully, they churn. The Accenture-ification of AI labs was inevitable; OpenAI just made it explicit. **Watch:** Whether Anthropic and Google follow with their own professional services arms.

**Microsoft Shopping for AI Startups Post-OpenAI**
Reuters reported Microsoft is actively acquiring AI startups to build its own model capability, preparing for life after the OpenAI partnership (revised April 27 to be less exclusive). No deals finalized, but the Inception AI acquisition is reportedly on the table. **Why it matters:** The Microsoft-OpenAI divorce is happening in slow motion. Microsoft spent $13B+ on OpenAI only to watch it become a competitor. Now they're hedging. The strategic logic is clear: Azure needs frontier models, and depending on a single partner that's building consumer products (and a consulting arm) is untenable. **Watch:** Which startup Microsoft actually acquires — it'll signal whether they want a research lab or a product team.

**GPT-5.5 Instant Becomes ChatGPT Default**
OpenAI rolled GPT-5.5 Instant to all users on May 5, replacing GPT-5.3 Instant. Sharper accuracy, better STEM, stronger web search, more concise outputs. API access went live April 24. **Why it matters:** The "Instant" tier is where most of ChatGPT's 300M+ weekly users live. This is quiet but massive — incremental improvements at this scale compound into real behavioral change. The goal-based prompting framework is particularly interesting for agent reliability.

**Google I/O 2026 — Tomorrow (May 19-20)**
Google front-loaded Android announcements at The Android Show (May 12): Gemini Intelligence with proactive AI on Android, Gemini in Chrome with "auto browse" agentic capabilities, Android XR glasses (partnerships with Samsung, Warby Parker, XREAL), and "Aluminium OS" — an Android-based ChromeOS replacement. The main keynote tomorrow is expected to feature Gemini 4.0, expanded cloud agentic APIs, and Google Search AI mode upgrades. **Why it matters:** Google is betting everything on Gemini as the unifying layer across Android, Chrome, Search, and enterprise. If Gemini 4.0 matches Claude Mythos on benchmarks, this week becomes Google's narrative to own. **Watch:** Gemini 4.0 benchmarks, pricing, and whether the enterprise agentic toolkit is genuinely differentiated from Anthropic's MCP ecosystem.

**Vercel Labs Drops "Zero" — A Programming Language for AI Agents**
Released May 15, Zero is an experimental systems language designed for AI agents to read, repair, and ship native programs. Sub-10KiB binaries, structured compiler output, explicit effects. Currently v0.1.1, Apache-2.0. **Why it matters:** This is philosophically interesting — instead of making AI better at existing languages, make a language AI is natively good at. Vercel already launched skills.sh ("npm for AI agents") in January. They're building the agent developer platform layer. **Watch:** Whether any serious agent framework adopts Zero, or if it stays an interesting experiment.

**Claude for Small Business — 15 Agentic Workflows**
Anthropic launched Claude for Small Business (May 13) — a toggle inside Claude Cowork connecting to QuickBooks, PayPal, HubSpot, Canva, DocuSign, Google Workspace, and M365 with 15 ready-to-run workflows. All actions require user approval. **Why it matters:** SMBs are 44% of US GDP. This is Anthropic going after the long tail that enterprise sales teams can't reach. The "approval required" design is smart — it addresses the trust gap that's killed every previous "AI for small business" pitch.

## 🪙 CRYPTO & DEFI

**CLARITY Act Clears Senate Banking Committee**
The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act passed the Senate Banking Committee markup on May 14 after a contentious session with 100+ amendments filed. The bill defines when crypto tokens are securities vs. commodities, includes the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act (non-custodial devs aren't money transmitters), and creates a regulatory framework the industry has wanted for years. Warren filed dozens of opposing amendments targeting DeFi and Fed master accounts. Most failed. **Why it matters:** This is the real deal. Committee passage opens a path to full Senate vote — the most significant crypto market structure legislation ever to get this far. Combined with the GENIUS Act (stablecoins), the US is building a comprehensive crypto regulatory framework for the first time. Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong publicly praised the bill. **Watch:** Senate floor vote timing and whether Warren's DeFi concerns gain enough traction to delay it.

**Starknet's strkBTC Goes Live — ZK-Shielded Bitcoin on L2**
strkBTC launched May 12 as planned — a Bitcoin-backed asset on Starknet using zero-knowledge cryptography to shield balances and transaction history. Users can toggle between public and shielded modes. Future upgrades target confidential lending, anonymous trading, and yield. **Why it matters:** Privacy is becoming the killer feature in crypto. CertiK reported 34 verified on-chain incidents in 2026 (up 41% YoY), with AI tools increasingly used to link public wallet addresses to physical identities. strkBTC directly addresses this threat. For Starknet, this is the narrative differentiator — "BTCFi with privacy" is a unique positioning no other L2 has. **Watch:** TVL growth post-launch and whether Nostra, Ekubo, and other Starknet DeFi protocols integrate strkBTC quickly.

**Meta Stablecoin Payments Going Live via Stripe**
Meta launched USDC stablecoin payouts to content creators on Solana and Polygon via Stripe. The broader plan: stablecoin payment rails across WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram commerce — learned from the Libra/Diem disaster, this time through a third-party provider rather than building their own coin. Warren has already raised concerns. **Why it matters:** Meta has 4B MAUs. Even a small percentage using stablecoin rails would dwarf the entire current stablecoin transaction volume. Meta paid $3B to creators in 2025 — shifting that to crypto rails reduces cross-border friction dramatically. This is the mass adoption moment stablecoin advocates have been waiting for. **Watch:** Regulatory response, especially given the GENIUS Act's stablecoin framework. Meta + stablecoins is a political lightning rod.

**Tokenized Treasuries Hit $15.35B Record**
Tokenized US Treasuries reached a record $15.35B in value locked as traders sought yield outside spot crypto amid Fed rate concerns. BlackRock's BUIDL fund leading the charge. **Why it matters:** Institutional capital is entering crypto through the boring door — yield, not speculation. $15B in tokenized T-bills is real money seeking real returns on-chain. This is the bridge between TradFi and DeFi that actually works.

**Hyperliquid Dominates Chain Fee Revenue — 43% Market Share**
Hyperliquid captured ~43% of all blockchain fee revenue last week, generating ~$11M primarily from perpetual futures. Three protocols (Hyperliquid, Pump.fun, EdgeX) distributed $96.3M to token holders in a single month. **Why it matters:** Fee revenue is the only honest metric in crypto. Hyperliquid is generating more revenue than most L1s combined, and it's flowing directly to holders. The "vertical chain" thesis — purpose-built chains for specific use cases — is winning against general-purpose L1s.

**BTC ~$80K, ETH ~$2,100-2,200 — Weak Week**
Bitcoin traded sideways around $80-81K before slipping to ~$78K by weekend. ETH dropped from $2,340 to ~$2,100-2,200, with RSI hitting oversold at 29.6. Hotter CPI data and rising Treasury yields pressured risk assets broadly. **Why it matters:** Crypto is trading like a macro asset, not a tech bet. The CLARITY Act catalyst wasn't enough to overcome inflation fears. ETH looks particularly weak — oversold technically but lacking a near-term catalyst beyond Starknet ecosystem activity.

## 🌍 MACRO & MARKETS

**Trump-Xi "Stalemate Summit" in Beijing (May 14-15)**
Trump visited China for the first time since 2017. Topics: Iran war, trade, Taiwan. The Guardian dubbed it the "stalemate summit" — Trump claimed they "settled a lot of different problems" but no concrete deals emerged. Trade tariffs remain, Taiwan tensions unchanged. **Why it matters:** The US-China relationship is the macro backdrop for everything — tech supply chains, chip export controls, AI competition. The NYT reported China sought access to Anthropic's latest models and was denied, underscoring how AI is now central to geopolitical rivalry. The market wanted a tariff de-escalation and didn't get one.

**Inflation Comes in Hot — Bonds, Stocks, Gold All Sell Off**
Core CPI surprised to the upside, PPI ran hotter than expected, and markets puked: S&P 500 closed at 7,408 on Friday (-1.24%), bonds sold off hard, silver dropped 7%, gold fell. The mid-week rally (Dow briefly above 50K for first time since Feb, S&P hit record on May 14) reversed sharply. HSBC raised its S&P target to 8,000 but acknowledged "lingering inflation and oil price risks." **Why it matters:** The inflation surprise killed rate cut expectations and raised fears of a possible rate *hike*. This is the macro regime shift everyone feared — AI-driven productivity gains haven't (yet) translated into disinflation. Oil prices elevated due to Strait of Hormuz uncertainty aren't helping.

**Iran Ceasefire on "Life Support"**
The US-Iran ceasefire technically holds but Iran hasn't fully reopened the Strait of Hormuz. Trump called the ceasefire "on life support" after rejecting Iran's latest proposal for lacking nuclear concessions. US Navy Operation Project Freedom continues escorting merchant ships through the Gulf. Vance said Iran is "promising to reopen" the strait but gave no timeline. **Why it matters:** ~20% of global oil transits the Strait. As long as it's partially blocked, oil stays elevated, inflation stays sticky, and the Fed can't cut. This is the single most underappreciated macro risk for tech and crypto markets right now.

**HSBC Raises S&P 500 Target to 8,000**
Despite the inflation scare, HSBC boosted its year-end target citing improving AI adoption, broader market participation, and "easing macro conditions" ahead. The bull case: we're in a tech/services economy where energy costs matter less than they used to. **Why it matters:** The tension between sticky inflation and AI-driven productivity is the central macro debate of 2026. If HSBC is right, the selloff is a buying opportunity. If inflation forces a rate hike, it's the beginning of something worse.
